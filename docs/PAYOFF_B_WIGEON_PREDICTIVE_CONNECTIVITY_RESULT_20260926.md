## Result

The wigeon information-axis prediction was **not supported**.

The historical ERA5 reconstruction produced 17 unique consecutive staging-pair
predictive-connectivity estimates from the pre-outcome period 2000–2017. Signed
detrended connectivity varied substantially:

- minimum rho = 0.208
- maximum rho = 0.912
- mean rho = 0.584
- transition-row SD = 0.179

The frozen 2018–2020 transition analysis contained 224 transitions from 28
individuals.

The preregistered term was:

[
E_{origin} \times z_{connectivity}.
]

Prediction:

[
\beta_{interaction}<0,
]

because higher pre-existing route predictability was expected to permit stronger
phase correction and therefore lower retention.

Observed:

[
\hat\beta=+0.0202,
\qquad SE_{cluster}=0.0643,
]

with

[
95\%\ CI=[-0.1058,0.1461],
\qquad p=0.756.
]

This is **NOT_SUPPORTED**. The point estimate is slightly opposite to the
registered direction and the interval is wide.

## Interpretation

This result rules out a simple claim that route-level predictive connectivity
is already a portable controller of phase correction across the existing
wigeon staging transitions.

It does **not** erase the earlier finding that wigeon phase error contracts
across transitions. It says that the amount of contraction is not detectably
stronger on staging pairs whose historical TGS timing is more predictable in
the registered analysis.

The Bayesian timing mechanism therefore cannot be presented as directly
confirmed by wigeon.

## Evidence consequence

The wigeon lane becomes a useful boundary case:

    phase correction exists
    +
    route predictive connectivity is measurable
    +
    registered connectivity x phase-error interaction is absent.

So phase correction and predictive connectivity are distinct empirical
quantities. If the broad-bird lane shows an information effect, PAYOFF-B must
frame that as a cross-species association rather than a universal within-route
controller.
