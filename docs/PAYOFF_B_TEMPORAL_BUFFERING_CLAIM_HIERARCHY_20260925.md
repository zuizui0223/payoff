# PAYOFF-B integrated ecology — temporal-buffering claim hierarchy

Frozen for the reframed PREOUTCOME manuscript: **2026-09-25**

## Primary ecological conclusion

> **Temporal buffering delays but does not permanently replace spatial tracking
> under sustained environmental change.**

This claim is supported by the declared synthetic moving-landscape programme:

- timing capacity expands the sampled persistence envelope;
- phenology can support a temporary low-movement / phenology-only regime;
- timing reduces the immediate sampled cost of fragmented spatial routes;
- movement re-enters under stronger sustained directional forcing;
- frontier strategies remain migration-dominant.

The mechanism is therefore **buffering followed by spatial re-entry**, not
permanent substitution of timing for movement.

## Ecological consequence

> **Low current mismatch can conceal latent spatial tracking demand.**

Here, *latent spatial tracking demand* is a descriptive phrase for the spatial
redistribution that becomes necessary to maintain tracking once finite temporal
adjustment is exhausted.

It is **not**:

- a newly fitted state variable;
- an empirically estimated quantity in the 55-species bird analysis;
- a directly measured cross-taxon trait;
- a claim that all natural phenological adjustment creates hidden movement
  demand.

The phrase summarizes the mechanism visible in the declared explicit-landscape
simulations.

## Empirical generality test

The 55-species analysis does not directly estimate latent spatial demand.

Its role is different and confirmatory with respect to the one-dimensional
alternative:

> **Natural phenological migration does not collapse onto one portable
> animal-speed/environmental-wave-speed optimum.**

This is consistent with tracking burden being distributed among movement,
waiting, timing, route geometry and other constraints rather than expressed on
one movement-speed axis.

## Direct-system evidence

Mule deer, barnacle geese and Eurasian wigeon provide mechanistic decomposition,
not three independent estimates of one controller.

They show that incoming phase error can be transformed in real migration
systems, while:

- ecological correction interval differs;
- actuator architecture differs;
- environmental reconstruction can change actuator inference;
- raw lambda remains segment-scale.

Thus the direct systems support **multiple realized control routes**, not a
universal lambda.

## Inference consequence

Only after the ecological mechanism is established do we make the inference
statement:

> **Mismatch is an outcome, not a tracking architecture.**

The logic is:

```text
timing can temporarily absorb tracking demand
-> finite capacity hides when movement becomes necessary
-> low mismatch can coexist with latent spatial demand
-> therefore mismatch alone does not identify mechanism or remaining capacity
```

The inference statement is not the headline result.

## Aikens boundary

The registered Aikens fixed-24 h perturbation asks whether independently
observed actuation attenuation propagates into phase retention within taxon.

PASS, wrong-direction, insufficient-support and NOT_ESTIMABLE outcomes all
leave the primary temporal-buffering conclusion unchanged.

No Aikens outcome may be used to retune:

- the moving-landscape buffering result;
- the 55-species speed-rule falsification;
- the title;
- the primary ecological conclusion.

## Claim ceiling

The integrated paper may claim that temporal buffering can conceal latent
spatial tracking demand **in the declared synthetic moving landscapes** and use
the natural datasets to reject a universal one-speed description and document
multiple realized phase-control routes.

It may not claim that latent spatial tracking demand has already been directly
measured across natural taxa or that all low mismatch in nature necessarily
contains hidden movement demand.
