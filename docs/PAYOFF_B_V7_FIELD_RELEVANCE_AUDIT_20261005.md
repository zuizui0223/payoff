# PAYOFF-B V7 field-relevance audit — 2026-10-05

Status: **PREOUTCOME FIELD-POSITION CHECK**

## 1. The ecological question is not invented by PAYOFF-B

Nemes et al. (2024, Journal of Animal Ecology) tracked 102 individual
insectivorous songbirds between southern and northern Motus receiver locations
and showed that birds catch up to the advancing spring wave while migrating
north.

In their Discussion, the authors explicitly identify the unresolved information
problem: environmental correlation along a route should determine how reliable
conditions at one location are as information about conditions farther ahead,
but **they did not measure phenological predictability** and therefore could not
assess how well Gulf Coast spring predicted spring farther north.

V7 takes this declared limitation literally.

The question is:

> **When a migrant encounters spring at a southern location, how informative is
> that spring about the conditions it will encounter farther north, and does
> that information structure predict how much seasonal lag the bird closes en
> route?**

## 2. Why migration ecologists care

The problem sits directly in migration ecology and information ecology.

Long-distance migrants must make sequential movement decisions before they can
directly observe all future environments. Earlier locations can be useful
predictors only when environmental states remain correlated across space.

Kölzsch et al. (2015) showed in an avian herbivore that predictability of spring
phenology differs among migration routes and matters for migration timing.

Bauer, McNamara & Barta (2020) formalized the general evolutionary problem:
migration timing depends not only on environmental variability but on the
reliability of information available to the migrant.

Nemes et al. then showed individual songbirds catch up to spring but left
route predictability unmeasured.

Thus V7 does not create a new theoretical concern. It connects an established
information-ecology question to an explicitly unresolved component of a public
individual songbird tracking study.

## 3. Important adjacent evidence

Bobowski et al. (2025, Scientific Reports) used a Common Yellowthroat genoscape
to show that Gulf Coast passage timing differs among breeding populations and
depends on breeding-ground spring onset, remaining migration distance and sex
once population structure is considered.

This establishes that downstream destination phenology is relevant to passage
timing and that population structure can confound simple timing relationships.

It does not measure paired within-individual downstream catch-up or historical
phenological predictability between sequential route locations.

V7 therefore includes species, year and route distance as compulsory
covariates and does not interpret a predictability association as cognition.

## 4. Negative evidence must remain visible

PAYOFF-B already ran a preregistered Eurasian wigeon test asking whether
predictive connectivity amplifies correction after incoming phase error.

That interaction was not supported.

V7 is not a rescue of that null:
- different taxon;
- different movement sampling;
- different environmental information geometry;
- different response (observed paired catch-up rather than a wigeon
  correction interaction).

A positive V7 result would imply that environmental predictability can matter
in this songbird route context, not that predictive connectivity is a universal
migration rule.

A null V7 result is scientifically interpretable and closes this route.

## 5. Why this is closer to the user's original PAYOFF-B question

The original biological intuition was:

- a migrant cannot directly know distant future spring when it first acts;
- it may acquire information while moving;
- the usefulness of early information depends on whether conditions here
  predict conditions there;
- being able to change speed is different from knowing which change is useful.

V7 measures exactly the third point rather than introducing another latent
controller or abstract flexibility index.

## 6. What V7 would add if supported

Not:
- "birds catch up to spring" — Nemes et al. already showed this;
- "environmental predictability matters in migration" — prior art;
- "remote cues exist" — prior art.

The narrow contribution would be:

> **Among individually tracked migrating songbirds, historical predictability
> between sequential route environments explains variation in how strongly
> birds close their phenological lag en route.**

This claim is only licensed if the predeclared source, coverage and robustness
gates pass.

## 7. Why the answer matters biologically

Two migration routes can experience the same mean advance of spring yet differ
in whether early-route conditions reliably forecast later conditions.

If catch-up depends on that reliability, climate vulnerability cannot be
inferred from migration distance or mean spring timing alone. What matters is
whether information available before the next decision remains predictive.

This provides a concrete interpretation of "information distance":
not kilometres by themselves, but the loss of environmental predictive
structure across those kilometres.

## 8. Stop rule

V7 is closed without reframing if:
- historical route predictability cannot be reconstructed for the frozen
  sample;
- the source-coverage gate fails;
- a direct prior-art collision is found;
- the H1 association is unsupported under the prespecified model and mandatory
  sensitivities.

No new theoretical layer is added to rescue a null.
