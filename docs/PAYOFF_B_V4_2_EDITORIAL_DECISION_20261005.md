## One ecological question

Why can better environmental predictability fail to produce better seasonal tracking?

## One answer

Predictive information and the opportunity to act on it are separate ecological
quantities. Seasonal information can become more accurate while the remaining
response set contracts.

## Three-part biological spine

1. Forecasting problem ("Schroedinger's spring"): the future seasonal state is
   not directly observable early in the trajectory.
2. Recourse problem ("Shinkansen"): an error at departure need not equal the
   error at arrival because speed, stopover, route, and later timing can still
   change.
3. Re-estimation problem ("Mikawa-Anjo clock"): intermediate checkpoints allow
   signed phase error to be re-estimated and corrected before the endpoint.

The analogies are explanatory devices only. Journal-facing text should use
forecasting, retained actionability/recourse, and repeated phase estimation.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Main empirical falsification

In the sampled eastern North American migratory-bird system, detrended
source-destination spring coupling strengthened between 2002-2009 and 2010-2017
(mean delta-rho +0.369). The positive contrast survives source and target
clustering, coarse spatial blocking, and calendar-year omission.

Larger connectivity gains did not predict larger reductions in
arrival-green-up mismatch. The apparent positive transfer slope is reproduced
by structural nulls and is not interpreted as a bird response.

Licensed ecological conclusion:
Broad degradation of this environmental predictive coordinate is not sufficient
to explain persistent mismatch in this system, and improved predictability did
not guarantee improved realized tracking.

Not licensed:
- climate change caused the increase in connectivity;
- birds perceived the fitted correlation as a cue;
- loss of actionability caused the bird result.

### Mechanism anchor

Mule-deer trajectories establish that post-entry correction is biologically
real and signed: phase variance contracts strongly along the route, late
individuals move faster and stop less, and early individuals show the opposite
response. This supports the existence of a downstream controller but does not
directly identify q(t), r(t), or the theoretical optimum.

## Theory headline

The main theoretical object is actionable information, not generic value of
information or optimal stopping.

For increasing cue quality q(t) and declining retained actionability r(t),
usable information value can peak at an intermediate stage. In the exponential
special case,

t* = log(1 + alpha/beta) / alpha.

With a positive deadline cost, information can be worth using only in a finite
entry-peak-exit window even while cue accuracy keeps improving.

## Novelty boundary

Do not claim novelty for value of information, sequential learning, optimal
stopping, irreversible action, or the fact that constraints matter.

The narrow contribution is the seasonal ecological geometry that jointly tracks
improving predictive information and declining biological actionability, then
links it to post-entry phase correction and interaction mismatch.

Theurich et al. (2026) is a useful nearby boundary: spring-departing Brent geese
show strong early-season tailwind selectivity that weakens late in the departure
window as urgency increases. This is consistent with declining late-stage cue
selectivity, but it does not independently estimate future-state information
quality and retained actionability or test the predicted intermediate maximum.

## Next empirical paper

A direct research-paper test should independently estimate q(t), retained
response opportunity r(t), and cue-linked behavior at at least three ordered
stages of the same seasonal decision. The strongest confirmation is an
entry-peak-exit pattern in cue use while cue accuracy continues to improve.

That test is an extension of the present paper, not a prerequisite for the
current American Naturalist submission route.
