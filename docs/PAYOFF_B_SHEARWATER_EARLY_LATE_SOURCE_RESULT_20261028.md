Twenty-two individual original GPS tracks passed both fixed elapsed-time windows: [0,12h) and [12,24h] since first valid GPS fix. Source rules required >=6 distinct fixes per window, sampled points near both boundaries, maximum 3h gaps inside a window and <=4h across the midpoint. One magnetic track failed 24h boundary coverage. Original 5 olfactory-arm Argos devices were assigned by design, not misclassified as five GPS instrument failures. GPS locations, exact dates and original animal IDs were not exported.

The original paper **already demonstrated** olfactory and coastal navigation (Pollonara et al. 2015, Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/srep16486). The present 22-animal sample permits calculation of a descriptive early-vs-late movement proxy; it is not an independent new manipulation, a route correction effect, direct cue receipt, a phenological phase shift, or a PAYOFF-B information × recourse confirmation.

**Critical identification boundary:** if a per-bird binary improvement is available only in 6 of 14 olfactory-arm subjects, the unobserved 8 give a worst-case treatment-arm frequency interval of width **8/14 = 0.5714**. For the control arm with 8 of 9, width **1/9 = 0.1111**. A worst-case olfactory-minus-control full-arm effect bound is therefore **0.6825 wide**, before observing any directional improvement value. More exclusions from actual route-quality gates could only widen it.

The next independent step needs a higher-powered, comparably instrumented cue-intervention cohort with actual cue onset, later route correction opportunity and a downstream ecological outcome. This document is a verified source-readiness result, not a new biological finding.
