# PAYOFF-B: cue-guided choice is not the (negative) value of information

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Prospective finite-state model + mathematical consistency test; synthetic only.  
**Related:** PR #323 information/state manipulation proposal; PR #322 BTBW annual prior-art benchmark. No previously frozen PAYOFF-B V3/V7R/V8 outcome is changed.

## 1. A necessary correction of the previous game interpretation

PR #323's wording "information-value sign reversal generated by an interacting competitor" is misleading if read as **negative optimal expected value of free, optional information with other actors' strategies held fixed**. Such a loss is impossible: the information-informed organism can always ignore the signal and use its old policy.

Formally, for predecision information I, new signal S, action set A unchanged and payoff W, the standard Bayesian value of additional information is

    VOI = E_S[ max_(a in A) E(W(a,H) | I,S)]
          - max_(a in A) E[W(a,H) | I] >= 0.

Proof: conditional expectation of each original policy is available after observing S. The informed optimum is at least as large as adopting the old I-based action for every realized S, so averaging cannot decrease expected payoff. This is **standard decision theory**, not a new PAYOFF-B theorem.

What *can* change sign is the cue-guided **entry versus avoidance advantage** as the competitor density or interaction costs change. A net negative realized value of providing a signal would require an explicit information-acquisition cost, obligatory response, behavioral bias, strategic reactions/crowding by other actors, or changed action sets. Those cases have different baselines and cannot be inferred from a one-actor payoff crossing.

## 2. Exact finite-state ecological choice model

Seasonal patch state H is good (probability p) or bad (1-p). Observed signal S matches the state with accuracy q in [1/2, 1]. An entering actor gets

    W(enter,H,d) = V_H - c*d,

where d is competitor density and c>0 the marginal competition cost. Avoiding the patch returns zero. Information may be ignored without a penalty. This can represent migrant nest-site selection, but is **not calibrated** to great tit / flycatcher observations.

Without a cue:

    W0(d) = max[0, p V_good + (1-p)V_bad - c*d].

With a cue available before the reversible decision:

    W1(d) = sum_(s in {good,bad}) max[0, sum_H P(H,s)(V_H - c*d)].

Then W1-W0 >= 0 under fixed d, c, action set and costs. If the cue arrives *after this one settlement commitment*, W1=W0 for this focal settlement action; later stages may still remain flexible, outside this toy model.

The **decision boundaries**, when their signal occurs with positive probability, are:

    d_good = E[V_H | S=good] / c,
    d_bad  = E[V_H | S=bad]  / c,
    d_0    = E[V_H] / c.

For q>1/2, 0<p<1, V_good>V_bad, we have d_bad < d_0 < d_good. Signal conditioning affects settlement only over the interval (d_bad, d_good), intersected with biologically possible d >= 0. Above d_good, even a "good" cue is not worth entry; **the positive information itself has not become harmful**, it has become useless for this focal choice.

## 3. Predeclared synthetic demonstration

For p=0.5, q=0.8, V_good=4, V_bad=-2, c=1,

| Competitor density | Optimal no-cue payoff | Optimal cue-informed payoff | VOI |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1.00 | 1.40 | +0.40 |
| 0.5 | 0.50 | 1.15 | +0.65 |
| 1.0 | 0.00 | 0.90 | +0.90 |
| 1.5 | 0.00 | 0.65 | +0.65 |
| 2.0 | 0.00 | 0.40 | +0.40 |
| 2.5 | 0.00 | 0.15 | +0.15 |
| 3.0 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

Values are arbitrary dimensionless payoff units, NOT estimates of bird reproductive success. The example predicts a **nonmonotone opportunity for information to change settlement**: around intermediate density the cue helps distinguish worthwhile from unprofitable patches; at sufficiently high density it changes nothing because both signals lead to avoidance. This is a **model-specific consequence**, not an original universal ecological principle.

The decline in VOI at high d is importantly different from negative VOI. Increasing q cannot make optimal expected payoff worse in this fixed-choice binary experiment.

### Route-stage counterexample: the ecological opportunity can close before the clock does

Let cue accuracy increase across a **six-stage synthetic journey** (0.60 → 0.95) while density at a prospective breeding patch increases (0 → 4). The individual can still revise its *focal patch choice* through stage 4, and is committed only at stage 5. The binary-choice calculation yields:

| Stage | q (accuracy) | d (competitor density) | Patch choice reversible? | Marginal VOI |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | 0.60 | 0.0 | yes | 0.00 |
| 1 | 0.70 | 0.5 | yes | 0.35 |
| 2 | 0.80 | 1.0 | yes | **0.90** |
| 3 | 0.85 | 2.0 | yes | 0.55 |
| 4 | 0.90 | 3.5 | **yes** | **0.00** |
| 5 | 0.95 | 4.0 | no | 0.00 |

**Nontrivial discrimination:** between stage 2 and stage 4, physical ability to select the patch is assumed constant and q increases, but *the improved information stops changing the optimal choice* because competition makes avoidance optimal under both cue states. This is **ecological crowding of the action incentive**, not information degradation or a missed physiological deadline. It offers a competing explanation for a hump in realized information-use value that should be tested against the earlier PAYOFF-B recourse-loss mechanism.

This example is an arbitrary parameter construction, not an estimated phenological process or a generic theorem: q, d and the payoff schedule must be independently estimated or manipulated before inferring this mechanism in a natural system. Settlement choice need not be the last action; later breeding or movement could still use late cues.

## 4. Distinctive ecological test is the timing × density × cue interaction

A new result would need evidence that, when seasonal cue changes **before** an actually reversible decision:

1. at a given actual resource state and resident density, changing the *signal* changes migrant choice;
2. the directional effect of an equally accurate signal depends on independent density / interaction risk and available later habitat;
3. after settlement commitment, signal presentation has no effect on that already completed decision, but may affect later reversible actions;
4. the action pattern is associated with *measured* adult/offspring success after contemporaneous settlement costs, not inferred from site choice alone.

A full H × S × cue-timing crossing can test cue-response behavior only if cue perception and assignment remain valid. **If S is randomized independently of H, S carries no objective information about H within the randomized experiment.** It can identify a response to a formerly reliable *symbolic/sensory cue*, especially before subjects learn the manipulation, but it cannot simultaneously establish rational, currently calibrated natural cue use. To test adaptive information use, additionally manipulate cue reliability or use a probabilistic rather than independent H-S design with a prespecified signal-observation model. Predeclare what organisms can plausibly learn and avoid treating a deceptive cue as an accurate forecast.

The earlier tit-hatching manipulation (Samplonius & Both 2017) changed true tit phenology and the social cue together, hence cannot identify their separate effects; it does establish that later-arriving females can respond at a different decision stage than early-arriving males. The fatal-competition study (Samplonius & Both 2019) shows that density/overlap and mortality interact, but lacks experimentally crossed information presentation. Joining their published effect sizes as if they are from the same randomized unit is not permissible.

## 5. Strong prior-art collision: NOT novel as a generic information-and-competition theory

Ecology has already developed information-use/competition dynamics and competitor-benefit tradeoffs, including:
- Parejo (2016), *Ecosphere*, doi:10.1002/ecs2.1295 — social information from competitors can benefit coexistence at some densities, with competition dominating at others.
- Gil, Baskett & Schreiber (2019), *Ecology*, doi:10.1002/ecy.2835 — information changes competition, coexistence/exclusion and extinction outcomes.
- The 2016 *Information use and resource competition: an integrative framework*, PMCID PMC4810826 — resource ecology affects the selection of social information strategies.
- Samplonius & Both (2017), doi:10.1111/1365-2656.12640 — manipulated tit timing affects late-arriving female flycatcher settlement.
- Samplonius & Both (2019), doi:10.1016/j.cub.2018.11.063 — tit density, timing overlap and lethal competition in migrants.

**PAYOFF-B cannot claim discovery of the competitor-as-informant idea, density-dependent advantages of information, or a generic information-value hump.** A new ecology-first contribution requires independent, time-valid cue and action measurements and a novel interaction/mortality consequence beyond these originals.

## 6. Admission / stopping rule for existing public data

Samplonius & Both 2017 Dryad DOI \`10.5061/dryad.bs427\` supplies:
- \`arrival.csv\`, \`arrorder.csv\`, \`population.csv\`, \`replicates.csv\`, \`swaps14.csv\`, \`swaps15.csv\`, \`arrival_early_late.R\`.
- The **published** setup manipulated tit brood timing, not independent actual phenology and cue display. The published authors explicitly did not distinguish habitat quality, predation and competition as proximate drivers.
- The code/data are good for independent *replication and source-field inspection* only; the design is **not** capable of estimating the full H × S × T causal model by adding interaction terms.

Samplonius & Both 2019 replication dataset is registered at doi:10.34894/85y1hy, but individual experimental cue-state-fitness linkage across 2017/2019 is **not established** by bibliographic overlap. Do not join them without verified individual/time identifiers and permissions.

Reopen an ecology-first causal claim only after verifying: actual plot-level cue timing, actual independent state and density, independently randomized cue manipulation, reversible decision time, and subsequent breeding success or survival in the **same** units with appropriate cluster replication. Absent such data, return \`MECHANISM_NOT_IDENTIFIED\`.
