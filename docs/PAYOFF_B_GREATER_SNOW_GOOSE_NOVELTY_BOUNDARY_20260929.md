# PAYOFF-B greater snow goose cue-uptake novelty boundary

Date: **2026-09-29**  
Status: **pre-outcome claim boundary**

## Prior work already owns the component ideas

### Local environmental cues can affect departure decisions

Bauer, Gienapp & Madsen (2008, *Ecology*, DOI 10.1890/07-1101.1) used
proportional-hazards models in pink-footed geese and showed that day length,
local accumulated temperature and their interaction predict departure, with the
importance of these variables changing along the spring migration route.

PAYOFF-B therefore must not claim that it newly discovers that:

- temperature affects goose departure;
- cue use can differ among stopovers;
- migratory decisions change en route.

### Information reliability can change optimal migration timing

Bauer, McNamara & Barta (2020, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*,
DOI 10.1098/rspb.2020.0622) developed migration theory in which environmental
variability and reliability of information alter migration timing decisions.

PAYOFF-B therefore must not claim novelty for the generic proposition that
reliable information has value for migration timing.

### Greater snow goose route predictability is already described

Reséndiz-Infante & Gauthier (2024, *Frontiers in Bird Science*,
DOI 10.3389/fbirs.2024.1307628) quantified temperature correlation across the
greater-snow-goose route. Predictability of later conditions was generally weak
from southern stages and stronger near Bylot; the paper also states that Arctic
stopover behavior and individual migration chronology remain incompletely
resolved.

PAYOFF-B therefore must not relabel these published route correlations as a new
discovery.

## The narrow empirical question that remains open

The preregistered GPS lane asks:

> **Does the behavioral effect of a currently observed local temperature cue
> become stronger where that cue has greater independently estimated,
> pre-outcome predictive value for a later ecological state?**

The primary estimand is therefore not a temperature main effect and not a
stopover-specific departure coefficient. It is

[
\beta_{
T_{local}\times predictive\ connectivity
}.
]

Predictive connectivity is estimated from historical climate years ending
before the focal migration year.

This distinction matters because the same local temperature anomaly can have
different informational meaning at two route contexts:

[
T_{local}
\quad\text{is observed in both places,}
]

but

[
P(\text{later Bylot state}\mid T_{local})
]

can differ.

The new test is therefore about **cue weighting by prospective information
content**, not the existence of cue-responsive migration.

## What would count as support

The strongest licensed natural statement after a positive primary result is:

> Local temperature is more strongly associated with next-day departure where
> historical source-to-future environmental connectivity is higher.

That supports a behavioral information-uptake axis predicted by PAYOFF-B.

It does **not** demonstrate:

- conscious assessment of cue reliability;
- the exact information-deadline threshold (q_{wait}(D));
- the delay cost (D);
- pairwise asynchronous information uptake;
- natural coordination hysteresis.

## What a null would mean

A null interaction would be informative rather than fatal to the broader
theory.

It would show that, in this greater-snow-goose system, route-level historical
predictive connectivity does not detectably modulate the local-temperature
departure association under the frozen analysis.

That outcome would be consistent with several alternatives:

- local temperature may be used as an immediate condition cue rather than a
  forecast of future conditions;
- photoperiod, endogenous programs, body reserves or other state variables may
  dominate departure timing;
- the relevant forecast target may be the next stopover rather than Bylot;
- population-level historical rho may be too coarse to represent information
  available to an individual bird.

No alternative may be promoted post hoc as the new primary hypothesis.

## Relationship to the information-deadline theorem

The GPS lane is an empirical bridge for

[
\text{information quality}
\rightarrow
\text{behavioral cue weighting}.
]

The exact theorem remains a separate stronger statement:

[
D_i
\rightarrow
q_{wait,i}
\rightarrow
\text{asynchronous information use}.
]

The GPS result may strengthen the first empirical bridge but cannot close the
second without independent (D), state-loss and actor-specific threshold
measurements.
