# PAYOFF-B endogenous information timing — frozen result

Frozen: **2026-09-26**

## Exact result

PAYOFF-B now treats information access as a timing decision.

The actor can:

    commit now under uncertainty

or

    wait for a more informative seasonal cue.

Waiting costs opportunity. In the canonical binary decision,

    prior early state = 0.40
    false-early loss  = 2
    missed-early loss = 1.

At cue accuracy 0.90, waiting reduces the focal actor's expected decision loss
by:

    V_private = 0.24.

If a wrong decision also imposes one payoff unit of mismatch loss on partners,
the system-level value of waiting becomes:

    V_joint = 0.54.

Therefore for any delay cost satisfying

    0.24 <= D < 0.54,

the focal individual rationally commits before the cue while the interacting
system would gain if the decision were delayed until the cue is available.

This is the **information-acquisition coordination wedge**.

## Endogenous information asymmetry

Actors can face the **same future cue** but different opportunity costs of
waiting.

For the canonical private information process:

    early actor delay cost = 0.30
    late actor delay cost  = 0.10.

On the sampled cue-accuracy grid:

- the lower-delay actor begins waiting at q = 0.82;
- the higher-delay actor does not begin waiting until q = 0.94.

Between those thresholds, information asymmetry arises without any difference
in sensory ability or cue reliability. One actor commits before information
arrives; the other waits and conditions its action on the cue.

Thus:

> **unequal information can be generated by unequal decision deadlines.**

## Source-backed ecological anchor

Samplonius & Both (2017) manipulated resident tit hatching phenology and
measured pied flycatcher settlement.

The experiment supplies a direct temporal contrast.

### Earlier male settlement

Male settlement among available nest boxes was not detectably related to tit
timing treatment:

    Z = 0.854
    P = 0.393.

The paper states that almost all males settled before the manipulated treatment
became apparent through tit hatching.

### Later female settlement

Female settlement produced a treatment signal. In the published male-pairing
GLMM:

    tit timing estimate = -0.101
    SE = 0.050
    Z = -2.030
    P = 0.042.

The time-dependent Cox analysis gives:

    treatment estimate = -0.065
    exp(coef) = 0.936
    SE = 0.025
    Z = -2.61
    P < 0.009.

Treatment separation became stronger later in the settlement period, around the
time early-treatment tits began hatching.

The original paper also reports that male and female arrival dates themselves
were unrelated to treatment. The experimentally supported contrast is therefore
between **when information became available** and **which later settlement
decision used it**, not an induced shift in migration arrival date.

## Strongest licensed interpretation

The experiment does not show that females intentionally waited to obtain tit
information, and it does not identify whether the effective cue was direct tit
phenology versus correlated food, predation or competition conditions.

The source-backed statement is narrower:

> **A heterospecific seasonal state can be unavailable to an earlier migrant
> decision and behaviourally relevant to a later one.**

That is enough to anchor the PAYOFF-B concept that information availability is
time-dependent.

## Prior-art boundary

Bauer, McNamara & Barta (2020; Proc. R. Soc. B,
DOI 10.1098/rspb.2020.0622) already model how environmental predictability and
intermediate stopovers affect the value of information for migration timing.

Schneeberger & Taborsky (2020; Functional Ecology,
DOI 10.1111/1365-2435.13488) explicitly emphasize that acquiring social
information carries time, energy and risk costs.

Value-of-information analysis itself is also established in ecology.

PAYOFF-B therefore does not claim novelty for costly information acquisition.

The narrower candidate contribution is:

    decision deadline
    x value of future information
    x interaction externality
    -> private-versus-joint incentive to wait
    -> endogenous information asymmetry
    -> downstream coordination and network memory.

## Relationship to the larger PAYOFF-B story

The information programme can now be ordered temporally:

    before commitment:
        predictive connectivity determines how much the migrant can know
        remotely;

    before cue visibility:
        opportunity costs determine whether it waits for better information or
        commits under uncertainty;

    after local arrival:
        heterospecific or environmental cues can update settlement decisions;

    after mismatch exists:
        phase-correction mechanisms act on realized error;

    across repeated strategic responses:
        interaction topology determines whether an information shock is erased
        or stored as ecological memory.

This is more specific than treating all of these processes as one generic
"phenological mismatch" mechanism.

## Information-induced desynchronization

The strongest counterintuitive result comes from allowing the two actors to
have different waiting costs while sharing the same future cue.

Canonical comparison:

    actor A delay cost = 0.30
    actor B delay cost = 0.10.

As cue reliability increases monotonically:

    q <= 0.81:
        commit | commit
        expected action mismatch = 0

    0.82 <= q <= 0.93:
        commit | wait
        expected action mismatch > 0

    q >= 0.94:
        wait | wait
        expected action mismatch = 0.

The mismatch peaks at the first threshold:

    q = 0.82
    expected mismatch probability = 0.436.

Thus better information has a non-monotone system consequence.

It first makes information valuable to only one partner, which breaks
coordination. Only after cue reliability becomes high enough for the
higher-delay partner to wait as well does coordination recover.

In compact form:

    shared ignorance
    -> asymmetric information use
    -> shared informed coordination.

The retained prediction is:

> **Improving information can transiently worsen interspecific timing mismatch
> when partners cross their value-of-information thresholds at different
> points.**

This is not the generic claim that information is harmful. The cue itself
improves monotonically. The temporary deterioration is generated by
asynchronous information uptake caused by unequal decision deadlines.
