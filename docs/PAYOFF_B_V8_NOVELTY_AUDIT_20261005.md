# PAYOFF-B V8 novelty audit — degradation of predictive connectivity

Date: **2026-10-05**

Status: **PREOUTCOME NOVELTY BOUNDARY**

## Candidate contribution

V8 asks whether the **cross-site environmental relationship itself** has
degraded across migratory routes:

> Does spring at an earlier location become a worse signed predictor of spring
> at a later destination through time?

The candidate novelty is a broad multi-species test of temporal change in this
cross-site predictive relationship.

## Prior art

### Kölzsch et al. 2015

Established that:
- onset of spring at consecutive stopovers can be spatially correlated;
- correlation / proportionality differs among migration steps and barriers;
- stronger predictability is associated with better migration timing.

Therefore V8 cannot claim novelty for spatial spring predictability or its
association with migration timing.

### Bauer et al. 2020

Established theoretically that environmental predictability alters optimal
migration timing and the value of intermediate information.

Therefore V8 cannot claim novelty for predictability as a migration mechanism.

### Robertson et al. 2024, PNAS

Across 150 Western Hemisphere migratory bird species:
- migration was generally more synchronized with long-term climatological
  green-up than with current-year green-up;
- green-up changed along migration routes over 2002–2021;
- changing green-up influenced migration–green-up mismatch.

This is close prior art for climate-driven decoupling, but it does not estimate
temporal change in source-to-destination interannual spring correlation.

### Wei et al. 2024, Global Change Biology

For 16 migratory herbivorous waterfowl species in Asia:
- spatially heterogeneous vegetation-phenology shifts under climate change
  reduced estimated migration-network integrity by about 12% on average.

This demonstrates spatially heterogeneous phenological change and migration
network disruption, but not temporal degradation of the signed correlation
between consecutive source/destination seasonal states.

### Schreven et al. 2026

Pink-footed geese provide direct route-level evidence that spring
predictability can change over decades.

The traditional Trøndelag–Svalbard step changed from uncorrelated in
1979–1999 to significantly negatively related in 2000–2022, while another
migration step also changed significantly.

Therefore V8 cannot claim novelty for the existence of climate-driven changes
or sign reversal in route-level spring predictability.

Schreven et al. are instead the strongest natural proof-of-concept motivating
the broad test.

## Search boundary

Targeted searches were run for:
- cross-site spring predictability trend in migratory birds;
- source–destination phenology correlation decline;
- climate change × migration-route environmental predictability;
- cue–future-state correlation through time;
- multi-species migration predictive-connectivity degradation.

The search recovered the studies above and general cue/predictability theory.

No published study was identified whose primary analysis tests temporal change
in detrended source-to-destination spring correlation across a broad
multi-species migratory-bird sample.

This is a search result, not proof of priority.

## Why this is biologically distinct

A spatially heterogeneous trend can change the mean timing difference between
sites without necessarily changing their year-to-year covariance.

Conversely, two sites can retain similar mean phenological trends while the
interannual relationship that makes one useful for forecasting the other
weakens or changes sign.

V8 targets the second quantity.

That distinction maps directly onto a migrant's information problem: a cue can
be locally informative about the current site yet become less reliable for
forecasting a later site.

## Allowed novelty wording before results

Allowed:

> Route-level studies show that spring predictability can change under climate
> warming, including sign reversal on a traditional Arctic goose route. We test
> whether such degradation of cross-site spring predictability is detectable
> across a broad multi-species migratory-bird sample.

Not allowed:
- "We discovered cue–environment decoupling."
- "We discovered that climate change shifts phenology differently in space."
- "We are the first to show migration follows climatological conditions."
- "We discovered that spring predictability matters to migrants."
- "All migration routes are losing information."

## Fail-closed rule

If a prior broad multi-species study is found that already estimates temporal
change in the same cross-site correlation object, V8 closes before focal
outcome analysis unless a biologically distinct prediction remains.
