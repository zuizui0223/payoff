# Greater snow goose fitness-loss anchor for PAYOFF-B

Date: **2026-09-29**  
Status: **source-backed ecological scale; not a direct estimate of theorem parameter D or C_M**

## Why this matters

The direct information-deadline test needs both an opportunity cost of waiting
and a fitness scale for making the wrong seasonal timing decision. The
greater-snow-goose literature now supplies a strong independent anchor for the
second quantity.

Reséndiz-Infante & Gauthier (2020, *Scientific Reports*,
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-78565-y) integrated breeding components from laying
through offspring survival to one year for the Bylot Island population over
1991–2015.

## Published late-timing fitness gradient

At the beginning of the study, predicted reproductive success peaked at
**0.52 young reaching one year** for birds laying on relative Day −4. It then
declined to **<0.01 young** by Day +10, an average post-peak reduction of
**0.036 young/day**.

At the end of the study, peak reproductive success was **0.74 young** on
relative Day −6 and fell to **0.01 young** by Day +10, an average reduction of
**0.046 young/day**.

Thus the absolute post-peak timing penalty became steeper over time. Expressed
only as a descriptive scale, the reported average declines correspond to about
6–7% of peak expected reproductive success per day. This normalization is a
calculation from the published values, not a separate fitted result.

The paper reports a **42% increase in maximum reproductive success of early
breeders** over 25 years while population median laying date did not advance
accordingly.

## An independently published waiting deadline

The same paper gives an explicit condition-dependent optimization example. A
female able to lay five eggs on Day −4 could benefit from waiting only briefly
to acquire nutrients for a sixth egg. Figure 4 gives a maximum delay of about
**1.6 days**; the text notes that if nutrient acquisition required **2 days or
more**, the expected reproductive success of waiting for the sixth egg would be
lower than laying five eggs immediately on Day −4.

This is not information acquisition, but it is a direct ecological example of

[
	ext{benefit of waiting}
quad	ext{versus}quad
	ext{fitness cost of delay},
]

with a finite deadline determined by the seasonal fitness surface.

## PAYOFF-B interpretation

This source strengthens two biological premises without closing the theorem.

### 1. Late-timing loss is large and measurable

The published reproductive-success surface provides a natural **C_M-like
timing-loss scale**. A delayed seasonal action can lose substantial expected
fitness within days.

It is not an exact (C_M), because the PAYOFF-B theorem declares binary states
and actions whereas the source estimates a continuous relative-laying-date
fitness surface. A future direct test must prespecify the early/late contrast
before mapping the source curve onto theorem utility units.

### 2. Waiting costs can create a real deadline

The 1.6–2 day nutrient-acquisition example independently demonstrates that
waiting can be beneficial only up to a finite time. This supports the
ecological meaning of PAYOFF-B's opportunity-cost term.

It is **not (D)** for the information game: the goose waits to acquire
nutrients rather than to acquire a later environmental cue.

## What this removes from the empirical gap

The greater-snow-goose lineage now contains separate evidence for:

[
	ext{future-state predictability}
]

from the route-temperature study,

[
	ext{D-like perturbation cost}
]

from migration captivity experiments, and

[
	ext{late-timing fitness loss}
]

from the long-term Bylot reproductive-success surface.

The remaining direct-theorem bottlenecks are therefore narrower:

1. identify a clean opportunity cost (D) in the same utility scale;
2. prespecify a binary early/late state-action contrast to obtain theorem-scale
   (C_F,C_M);
3. observe actor-level cue-use switching across enough pre-outcome (q)
   variation;
4. for the pairwise theorem, observe two interacting actors with distinct
   thresholds and the predicted asynchronous interval.

The current GPS cue-uptake lane addresses item 3 only at the
behavioral-information axis and remains explicitly unable to identify
(q_{wait}(D)).
