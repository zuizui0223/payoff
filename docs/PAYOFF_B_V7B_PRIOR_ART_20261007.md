## Established work

### IRG / green-wave literature

Bischof et al. and subsequent green-wave work established instantaneous rate of green-up (IRG), the time derivative of satellite vegetation greenness, as a phenology / forage-quality coordinate.

V7B does not claim IRG itself as new.

### Shariatinajafabadi et al. 2014

The same barnacle-goose populations were linked to MODIS-derived green-wave index, and goose arrival timing tracked the date of 50% GWI.

V7B therefore does not claim the first remote-sensing connection between local vegetation phenology and these geese.

### Ortega et al. 2023

Mule deer compensated for phenological mismatch by changing movement rate and stopover use.

Their reported cue-reliability analysis examined whether dates of peak green-up propagated spatially along the migration route. This is a route-scale forecast / propagation coordinate.

That paper also leaves unresolved the mechanism by which migrants identify their own temporal position relative to the green wave.

V7B does not claim first evidence of temporal compensation.

## Candidate gap

The targeted search did not identify a natural migration study whose focal test jointly combines:

1. a behavior-independent measure of the **local temporal readability / steepness** of the seasonal environmental cue at a route checkpoint;
2. independently estimated remaining temporal recourse; and
3. a direct corrective actuator response to incoming temporal mismatch.

This is a search boundary, not proof of priority.

## Novelty firewall

If a prior study is found before V7B outcome opening that directly tests the same local-readability × recourse interaction on corrective migration behavior, close V7B as prior-art collision.

Do not rescue novelty by changing the vegetation metric after outcome access.
