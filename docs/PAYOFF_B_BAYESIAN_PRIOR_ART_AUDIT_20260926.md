# PAYOFF-B Bayesian timing extension — prior-art audit

Date: **2026-09-26**  
Status: **novelty boundary for exploratory mechanism**

## What is already established

PAYOFF-B must not claim novelty for any of the following ingredients by
themselves.

### Game-theoretic phenology

Johansson & Jonzen (2012, Ecology Letters, DOI
10.1111/j.1461-0248.2012.01812.x) already showed that frequency-dependent
selection can stabilize phenologies that differ from the simple
fitness-maximizing timing and that climate change can generate multiple
eco-evolutionary trajectories.

Therefore the novelty claim cannot be "game theory applied to phenology."

### Informational mismatch under climate change

Parejo (2016, Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution, DOI
10.3389/fevo.2016.00031) explicitly framed climate-driven informational
mismatch as a threat to interspecific interactions.

Therefore the novelty claim cannot be "climate change can make ecological
information misleading."

### Migrants using predictive environmental connectivity

Burnside et al. (2021, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2026378118) showed in Asian
houbara that local departure temperature was correlated with conditions across
the migration system and could support adaptive shifts in migration timing.

Therefore the novelty claim cannot be "migrants use remote environmental cues"
or "departure-to-destination predictability matters."

### Hysteresis in ecological phenology

Zou et al. (2024, Nature Communications, DOI
10.1038/s41467-024-48676-5) showed alternative forest leaf-phenology states and
history dependence generated by positive demographic feedback.

Therefore the novelty claim cannot be "phenological states can show
hysteresis."

### Bayesian eco-evolutionary games under noisy environmental information

Patra, Bairagya & Chakraborty (2025, arXiv:2504.02399) explicitly formulate
Bayesian eco-evolutionary game dynamics in which players perceive a changing
environment through a noisy information channel.

Therefore the novelty claim cannot be "Bayesian games with noisy environmental
information are new to ecology."

### Resident--migrant phenological interaction effects

European tit--flycatcher analyses have already shown stronger temperature
sensitivity in resident tits than migratory flycatchers, divergence in laying
dates, and an additional association between resident timing and migrant timing
after controlling for temperature (Global Change Biology 2018,
PMID 29691942).

Therefore the novelty claim cannot rest on "resident and migrant phenology
respond differently" or on the existence of phenological interaction effects
between those groups.

## Narrow candidate contribution

The PAYOFF-B candidate contribution is the conjunction of mechanisms that are
normally studied separately:

    information quality of one migratory partner
    x interaction-dependent timing payoffs
    x multiple Bayesian timing equilibria
    x historical accessibility
    ->
    timing cascade into partners whose own information did not deteriorate
    ->
    persistent lower-joint-payoff timing regime after migrant information recovers.

The key distinguishing prediction is therefore **cross-species information
contagion with recovery hysteresis**.

A migrant's predictive connectivity can deteriorate while the flower and local
pollinator retain the same cue accuracy. Nevertheless the residents may change
their timing policy because the migrant changes the payoff of matching versus
following their own cue. Restoring the migrant's information need not restore
the original timing regime.

## Stronger discriminator from the robustness map

The preliminary phase diagram adds two non-monotone predictions that are not
generic consequences of cue unreliability:

1. **good-but-imperfect resident information window** — near-perfect resident
   cues resist the migrant-induced cascade, while intermediate-high resident
   cue quality can still be pulled into an inefficient historical basin;
2. **transition-prior window** — the strict hysteresis is strongest while early
   spring is plausible but not yet common enough to make early action
   unconditional.

These predictions, rather than the existence of uncertainty itself, should be
used to distinguish PAYOFF-B from existing informational-mismatch theory.

A third discriminator comes from the network-topology robustness result:

3. **topology-dependent ecological memory** — two sparse networks with the same
   edge count can both transmit a temporary migrant information shock, while
   only the network containing a resident--resident link retains substantial
   recovery hysteresis in the canonical contrast. Thus transmission and memory
   are different network properties.

## Current novelty wording

Safe wording:

> PAYOFF-B predicts that transient degradation of predictive connectivity in a
> mobile interaction partner can alter the timing policy of locally informed
> partners and leave the interaction network trapped in a lower-joint-payoff
> timing regime after predictive connectivity recovers.

Avoid:

- "first Bayesian model of phenology";
- "first game-theoretic model of climate phenology";
- "first demonstration of informational mismatch";
- "first ecological hysteresis in phenology.";
- "first Bayesian eco-evolutionary game with environmental noise";
- "first resident--migrant phenological interaction model."

## Empirical implication

A decisive test needs repeated observations of:

1. the migrant's pre-commitment cue;
2. the future destination state/resource phase;
3. local partner phenology;
4. interaction strength or dependence;
5. years spanning both deterioration and recovery of predictive connectivity.

The central empirical predictor is not migration distance. It is the
time-varying predictive relationship between the information available at
commitment and the later destination state.
