# PAYOFF-B direct-test screen for continuous information × actionability

Date: **2026-10-02**  
Status: **prospective source screen; frozen GEB Paper 2 unchanged**

## Target

The continuous prospective theorem is

    F(t) = r(t)[S q(t)-B] - D(t)

above the canonical information-actionability kink, with interior condition

    S q'(t) / [S q(t)-B]
      =
      -r'(t)/r(t)
      + D'(t)/{r(t)[S q(t)-B]}.

A direct natural test therefore needs the following quantities in one system:

| quantity | required meaning |
|---|---|
| q(t) | pre-commitment information quality at repeated stages |
| r(t) | independently justified retained actionability, not merely route progress |
| D(t) | cumulative effective cost of waiting, including compensation consequences |
| commitment/action | observed decision affected by the cue |
| repeated stages | enough support to locate or bracket a commitment optimum |

## Candidate screen

### 1. Greater snow goose — strongest prospective same-system candidate

**Information**

Already frozen before movement outcomes are opened:

- St. Lawrence -> Bylot historical temperature predictability;
- Nunavik -> Bylot;
- Baffin -> Bylot;
- rolling pre-focal NARR reconstruction;
- Gaussian sign-agreement q bridge retained only as a secondary mapping.

Published source anchors include approximately:

- St. Lawrence long window: rho=0.03 -> q~=0.510;
- St. Lawrence short window: rho=0.25 -> q~=0.580;
- Nunavik: rho=0.11 -> q~=0.535;
- Baffin: rho=0.35 -> q~=0.614.

The focal rolling q definition remains the preregistered pre-outcome NARR
construction, not these published static values.

**Observed commitment**

A preregistered GPS lane tests departure in the next 24 h from St. Lawrence,
Nunavik and Baffin.

**Downstream response**

A separately preregistered lane tests:

- downstream transit compression;
- whether compression is stronger after longer waiting;
- route skipping.

The focal event data remain unopened.

**Waiting-cost / fitness anchors**

The same population has:

- experimental captivity-duration effects on breeding;
- natural two-sided timing-fitness surfaces on Bylot;
- historical departure-to-arrival compensation;
- post-arrival pre-laying buffering.

**Blocking issue**

No existing quantity is a justified r(t).

Remaining route distance, number of route contexts, transit duration and
stopover duration are all biologically relevant but cannot be equated with the
reduced-form retained-actionability weight.

**Classification**

    STRONGEST_PROSPECTIVE_JOINT_INFORMATION_COMPENSATION_LANE
    FULL_CONTINUOUS_Q_R_TEST_NOT_YET_QUALIFIED

### 2. Barnacle goose — strongest existing two-axis descriptive system

Kölzsch et al. (2015) tracked 40 barnacle geese on three flyways and grouped
stopovers into 16 general regions.

The source paper directly relates consecutive-stopover environmental
predictability to arrival timing: geese arrived closer to local spring onset
where predictability was higher.

The repository independently reconstructs route-stage phase retention:

- Svalbard highlighted transition: lambda=-0.106;
- Greenland: lambda=0.131;
- Barents: lambda=0.494;

and reproduces negative stopover responses across independent environmental
surfaces for the highlighted barnacle transitions.

The broader existing transition screen did **not** support the simple prediction
that higher predictability produces stronger reactive correction
(Spearman rho=-0.464, p=0.294 across seven small/non-independent matched pairs).

This is useful evidence that:

    prediction before error
    !=
    correction after error.

But lambda is a phase-retention response coordinate, not r(t).

**Classification**

    STRONGEST_EXISTING_TWO_AXIS_DESCRIPTIVE_SYSTEM
    DIRECT_R_NOT_IDENTIFIED

### 3. Eurasian wigeon — strongest preregistered separation test

The system has:

- 224 consecutive staging transitions from 28 individuals;
- historical predictive connectivity for 17 route pairs;
- phase contraction across transitions.

The preregistered predictive-connectivity x incoming-phase interaction was:

    beta = +0.0202
    95% CI = [-0.1058, 0.1461]
    p = 0.756
    NOT_SUPPORTED.

Thus route predictability did not detectably strengthen post-error correction.

**Classification**

    PREREGISTERED_INFORMATION_VS_CORRECTION_SEPARATION
    DIRECT_R_NOT_IDENTIFIED

### 4. Mule deer — strongest signed-recourse system

Ortega et al. (2023) provide direct evidence that both signs of phase error
produce opposite downstream control:

- ahead animals decelerate / use longer stopovers;
- behind animals accelerate / use shorter stopovers.

The system strongly identifies recourse behavior but lacks an independently
calibrated pre-commitment q trajectory comparable to the theorem.

**Classification**

    STRONG_RECOURSE_WEAK_Q_FOR_CURRENT_PURPOSE

### 5. Bar-tailed godwit

Earlier population departure was absorbed by longer later Yellow Sea stopovers,
while Alaska arrival did not shift detectably.

This demonstrates stagewise timing transformation but does not identify q(t).

**Classification**

    STAGEWISE_BUFFERING_NO_Q

### 6. Plant-pollinator systems

Existing sources establish:

- unequal partner temperature sensitivity despite shared local exposure;
- flowering/activity-window duration as a potential buffer;
- direction of partner sensitivity can reverse among systems.

However temperature sensitivity is not cue reliability q, and flowering
duration is not automatically r.

**Classification**

    LOCAL_INTERACTION_BUFFERING_PLAUSIBLE
    Q_AND_R_NOT_ON_COMMON_SCALE

## Current conclusion

No existing natural dataset in the repository licenses a direct estimate of

    F(t)=r(t)[S q(t)-B]-D(t)

with all components independently identified.

The strongest evidence-supported statement is instead:

> Seasonal tracking contains at least two empirically separable control
> channels: prediction before commitment and correction after mismatch. Natural
> systems differ in how much they rely on each channel.

## Next valid empirical move

Do **not** create r from route distance or number of remaining stopovers.

For a direct reduced-form actionability test, r must be linked to the biological
meaning used in the theorem:

> the fraction/weight of otherwise comparable states in which information
> arriving at that stage can still change the focal action.

Three defensible routes are possible.

### A. Experimental actionability calibration

Manipulate the same cue at multiple stages while holding state comparable.
Estimate the fraction or probability of action changes caused by the cue.

This is closest to the theorem's reduced-form r.

### B. Structural decision model

Specify the feasible action set, action-state losses and cue likelihoods
independently, then derive r only as a summary of the resulting action-value
envelope.

This is stronger than interpreting physical remaining distance as r.

### C. Keep natural systems in two-axis form

Use q-like information measures and independent phase-correction / buffering
coordinates without forcing them into one scalar r.

This is currently the most defensible route for barnacle goose and wigeon.

## Immediate registered opportunity

The greater-snow-goose movement response remains unopened, so the existing
preregistrations remain the cleanest next empirical test:

1. execute the frozen cue-uptake lane;
2. execute the frozen downstream transit-compression lane;
3. report whether information-linked departure and information-linked
   compensation are both present;
4. **do not** back-solve r or D_eff from those associations.

If both are supported, the system becomes a strong natural demonstration that
one environmental cue affects both commitment and downstream compensation.
That would support the biological architecture motivating the continuous model,
while still falling short of a direct q(t)-r(t)-D(t) theorem test.
