# PAYOFF-B greater snow goose source-hypothesis audit

Date: **2026-09-29**  
Role: **literature-positioning note; does not alter the frozen focal analysis**

## The source paper already proposes the behavioral mechanism qualitatively

Reséndiz-Infante & Gauthier (2024) concluded that temperature encountered early
in the greater-snow-goose spring migration is generally a weak predictor of
later Arctic / breeding conditions, whereas local temperature becomes more
informative near the breeding grounds.

In the Discussion they make a specific behavioral suggestion for the last
migration stage: once near Baffin Island, geese might overfly the last staging
area and speed migration when spring is early, or stop and wait when spring is
late.

That statement is important for PAYOFF-B because it distinguishes two levels:

1. **published ecological interpretation:** near-destination temperature may
   contain useful forward information and could alter stop/wait behavior;
2. **unresolved behavioral test:** whether individual departure decisions are
   actually more contingent on a local temperature cue when its independently
   estimated forward predictive value is higher.

The PAYOFF-B GPS lane targets level 2.

## What the source tracking data were used for

The same paper reports that 2019–2022 tracking information was available and
was used to confirm broad periods of goose presence in Nunavik and Baffin.

It was not presented as an individual-level hazard analysis of:

[
local\ temperature
\times
historical\ forward\ predictability
\rightarrow
next\text{-}day\ departure.
]

Thus the PAYOFF-B model is not a reanalysis of an already reported coefficient.

## Precise contribution of the GPS lane

The frozen primary model converts the source paper's qualitative behavioral
idea into a falsifiable prospective prediction:

[
\beta_{
local\ temperature
\times
preoutcome\ predictive\ connectivity
}>0.
]

The key addition is not another test that temperature affects migration.
Bauer et al. (2008) already demonstrated that environmental predictors of goose
departure change en route.

The addition is a test of whether **cue weighting covaries with the prospective
information content of that cue about a later ecological state**.

## Claim ceiling

Even if supported, PAYOFF-B should write this as:

> Individual departure behavior was more strongly associated with local
> temperature in route contexts where historical local conditions were more
> predictive of later Bylot conditions.

It should not say that geese consciously estimate correlation, calculate a
forecast, or directly implement the model's exact (q_{wait}(D)).

A null result should remain equally publishable as a boundary result: the
published qualitative overfly/wait interpretation would then lack support under
the frozen individual-level predictive-connectivity test.
