# PAYOFF-B novelty boundary: 2025 time constraint × wind selectivity already published

**Audit date 2026-11-20. Published prior art, not newly fitted PAYOFF empirical evidence.**

Klinner, Karwinkel, Packmor & Schmaljohann (2025), *Movement Ecology* 13:64, published September 22, DOI [10.1186/s40462-025-00575-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-025-00575-0), open original full text [PMC12455787](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12455787).

## Confirmed original ecological claim

The study tracked the night-to-night departure decisions of **five** spring stopover songbird species on Helgoland: **three trans-Saharan migrants** (Common Redstart, Garden Warbler, Northern Wheatear), and **two pre-Saharan migrants** (European Robin, Dunnock). It reported **883 at-risk bird-night records before excluding seven meteorological records** where wind/crosswind made the specified flow-assistance value undefined; in the meteorology-containing Cox models **876** records were usable. These are bird-night observations nested in a few species, **not** 876 independent species or population replicates.

The paper's main result: **pre-Saharan migrants stopped longer and were more selective for favourable tailwind during spring departure**, whereas three trans-Saharan species showed less selective weather-related departure. Lower cloud cover increased departure likelihood across species. The interpretation was **lower time constraint for shorter-route pre-Saharan migrants**, and greater time urgency for long-distance migrants. Differences in migratory urgency were **inferred from strategy group**, *not randomized, independently measured future payoff or an experimentally induced information deadline*. The authors explicitly noted unknown geographic generalizability. Original article also reports energy-store effects on the first night and departure direction comparisons, all as prior art.

The paper states original full supplemental data/code at **DARE DOI 10.57782/WXZE6H**; original source raw bytes, individual ID joins and checksum **have not been independently inspected in PAYOFF**. No new regression effect, direction-specific p-value, or refitted coefficient is presented here.

| Natural perfect-information coordination trap / recovery hysteresis | Not observed in these source-level studies | Remains theory only |

## Mathematical and biological distinction

The theoretical ecological inference must not collapse into a generic claim that migratory birds 'sometimes respond less to weather when time-constrained'. That broad phenomenon is established prior art. A publishable strong PAYOFF-B natural-test result would need to show that even when future conditions are objectively predictable, **a cue arriving after the actionable decision deadline cannot compensate for reduced movement options**, with observed, independently defined cue reliability/time and action set and a valid downstream outcome; partner asynchrony/hysteresis requires still more original interacting-organism data.

The 2025 Klinner comparison **does not itself causally demonstrate Bayesian information value**: relative weather selectivity may reflect physiological differences, species' migration programmes, available routes, perceived weather, and different observation/detection processes. Nor can it simply be crossed numerically with the Lameris 2025 resource experiment, because those are distinct individuals, source sites, years and outcomes.

**Research action:** retain mathematical exact claims and already-supported component evidence, eliminate novelty language for urgency×tailwind selectivity, and prioritize a prospectively measured joint q×r experiment/source rather than more reanalyses of published weather coefficients.
