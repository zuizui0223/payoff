## Frozen secondary question (before inspecting GloPL numerical means)

How does the effect of supplemental pollen differ between the early and late parts of a flowering season within the same population? This measures **seasonal variation in experimental external pollen-service limitation**, not a partner-relative timing perturbation, endogenous recourse, or a demonstrated constraint on lifetime fitness. The early/late contrast is signed (late minus early) and no general directional prediction is imposed. It is not an amendment of primary theta. The original 10-independent-cluster threshold is not relaxed.

2. First source-confirmed paper: Totland & Schulte-Herbrüggen 2003, Arctic Antarctic and Alpine Research DOI 10.1657/1523-0430(2003)035[0242:BSIFVA]2.0.CO;2. Original methods directly describe high-elevation C. alpinum early 12–13 July versus late 20–21 July 2000; C. cerastoides early 22–23 July versus late 10 August 2000. At each period/site flowers on separate individuals were randomly assigned to four treatments, including natural and unbagged cross-pollen supplementation. Plants of both timing groups within each high-elevation population were spatially intermingled. Seed-to-ovule ratio is the measured endpoint (Figure 1, reported SE, 9–15 flowers per bar). Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1657/1523-0430%282003%29035%5B0242%3ABSIFVA%5D2.0.CO%3B2 . Exclude low altitude. Require exactly one early and one late row per source species; same population, supplementation level, emasculation context, study-year metadata and source design after replacing exactly one standalone early/late marker. An absent year in GloPL is acceptable only because the original paper explicitly specifies the common 2000 field season. If source labels cannot unambiguously resolve the high-elevation early/late pair, HOLD, no row selection by mean or precision.
3. Use only SO (seed/ovule), both periods with positive finite means (0<mean<=1), valid integer sample size >=2, finite SD >=0 if present. Point effect L_t=ln(Sup_X_SO/Natural_X_SO); delta=L_late-L_early. No pseudo-count. If either period SD/n is missing, point-only. For independent plant units within each period per original methods, v_t = SD_sup^2/(n_sup*mean_sup^2)+SD_nat^2/(n_nat*mean_nat^2). Do not assume zero covariance between periods: the original paper does not explicitly exclude repeated plants across periods. Report conditional zero-covariance sensitivity vE+vL and maximum variance (sqrt(vE)+sqrt(vL))^2 allowed by Cauchy-Schwarz given marginal variances, with approximate normal intervals. These are uncertainty sensitivities, not recovered covariance or robust exact small-sample coverage. Both species in one paper, no pooled estimate. Do not use GloPL published effect-size column or change endpoint to get a usable contrast.
