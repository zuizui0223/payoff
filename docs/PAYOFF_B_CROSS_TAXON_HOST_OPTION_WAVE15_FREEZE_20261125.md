## Primary candidate and prior-art collision

Pardikes et al. 2022, Global Change Biology 28:4013–4026, https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.16195. Final published abstract directly establishes alternative host species can widen the range of phenological shifts allowing interaction persistence, with physiological limitations under warming. This is material prior art against novelty claims that redundancy buffers phenological shifts or that thermal stress limits such buffering. Authorea June 2021 preprint https://doi.org/10.22541/au.162454818.82806593/v1 (public text https://d197for5662m48.cloudfront.net/documents/publicationstatus/65471/preprint_pdf/80b467ab082e48843fcdc644ac9fa4e9.pdf) describes parasitoid introduction at 0/2/4/6 days after host initiation, 24/28 C, 2/20 ml food, 100 eggs total in each vial, two single-host species or a 50/50 mixture of Drosophila birchii and D. sulfurigaster, two parasitoid species, five time blocks, 48 h parasitoid exposure. The preprint reports single-generation experimental survival and parasitism, plus multi-generation simulated coexistence. Keep versions and endpoint layers separate: do not treat simulated persistence as experimental recruitment. The 2021 preprint expressly says alternative hosts dampened differences across temperature/resource competition but not phenological shifts in single-generation outcomes; do not infer a positive signed recruitment interaction from the final abstract's persistence claim. No original file archive identified in current public literature/GitHub searches; screenshots failed; no extracted final table or fitted effect. HOLD pending the final paper and original vial-level adult parasitoid/host counts, control vials, denominators and block labels.

Proposed extraction if source becomes available: parasitoid emerged offspring per initially introduced female (counts per vial, zero valid), two mixture-vs-single contrasts and equal-density 50:50 monoculture reference, four delay levels, temperature and food strata, shared controls and time-block structure. Require actual allocation and adequate support; distinguish a changed host composition from simple additive richness. A D=delay vs zero interaction is not automatically rescue if zero-delay outcome is already poor or harmful. No change to main admission rule before source. Do not pool host survival with parasitoid recruitment or simulated persistence.

## Fixed host-option transfer analysis (separate secondary lane)

Weir 2024, Oecologia 206:225–239, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-024-05629-5. Source Methods: four populations, nine plant taxa, 100 randomly assigned larvae/population×host in five cultures of 20, mixed broods, host treatment start staggered to standardized young foliage stage. Larvae reared in groups, developmental time and pupal mass cannot be matched to particular individuals by design; do not join endpoint rows even if labels appear to match. Primary outcome for this *secondary assay* is observed survival to pupation (binary survp) from pupal_mass sheet. Require both sheets exactly 3600 rows, four populations, nine expected host codes, 100 rows per pop-host, exactly five culu cultures of 20 nested within pop-host, nonmissing 0/1 survival, unique ind within each sheet, agreement of the culture survival totals between sheets. Check original key dictionary; if grain or coding ambiguous, HOLD. No individual-level join of development time and mass. Original author notes source treatment broods may be shared; culture bootstrap cannot resolve brood covariance. Use only survival. No fecundity proxy reconstruction or other endpoint chosen by results.

- Fixed performance matrix S[p,h] = equally weighted mean of five culture survival proportions, 4x9. Record counts and all host contrasts relative to oak (Quercus). Record host rankings per population and count non-oak hosts with survival greater/equal/lower than oak. No inference of field preference or availability.
- Leave-one-population-out rule: train on the other three equally weighted populations, rank all eight non-oak hosts by survival (alphabetical tie break), then evaluate on held-out population. Enumerate all equally likely subsets of k=1,2,4,8 accessible alternative species. For each subset evaluate (i) blind uniform selection mean, (ii) training-ranked best available host on held-out performance, (iii) held-out oracle max as a descriptive upper bound (not attainable selection evidence). Equal weighting of subsets/populations. Record fraction of subsets exceeding held-out oak survival, all signs and values. This is a hypothetical option-value calculation, not observed dispersal or an experimental manipulation of choice. Availability assumption is deliberately artificial; host identity partly confounded with calendar/foliage collection differences. No actual mismatch, no behavior, no primary theta.
- Resample whole cultures separately within pop-host 10000 draws for conditional sensitivity of fixed host contrasts and the cross-validated rules; refit training rankings within each bootstrap draw to avoid fixing selected labels. No resampling of larvae, no claim of independent broods or representativeness of four populations. Report these only as culture-level conditional sensitivity intervals (if support verified), not causal estimates, prospective or geographic prediction guarantees. Report leave-one-population-out results themselves; no pooling across studies. No post-outcome design changes. All prior negative source holds retained.
