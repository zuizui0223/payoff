# PAYOFF-B cryptic clock-portfolio novelty boundary — 2026-10-03

Status: **post-freeze literature boundary; frozen GEB V2 unchanged**

## Prior concepts that are not new

Ecology and evolution already contain broad versions of the idea that similar
phenotypes can conceal different mechanisms.

### Genetic compensation / cryptic variation

Countergradient and genetic-compensation theory explicitly allows populations
with different genetic or physiological mechanisms to express similar field
phenotypes, with differences becoming visible in altered environments.

Cryptic genetic variation likewise concerns hidden mechanistic differences that
have little phenotypic effect under familiar conditions but become expressed
after environmental or genetic perturbation.

PAYOFF-B therefore must not claim novelty for the generic statement:

> similar historical phenotypes can hide different mechanisms that become
> visible after environmental change.

### Phenological mechanism heterogeneity

Phenology research has long emphasized that plants, insects and vertebrates can
use different timing mechanisms and therefore need not shift at the same rate
under climate change.

PAYOFF-B must not claim that different phenological mechanisms across taxa are
new.

### Behavioral flexibility and habitat-loss dependence

Migration literature already shows that the consequences of staging-area or
stopover loss depend on behavioral flexibility and availability of alternative
habitat.

PAYOFF-B must not claim that flexible migrants can nevertheless be vulnerable
to habitat loss as a new verbal idea.

### Recent phenology and migration work that narrows the claim

Recent literature makes several adjacent ideas explicit.

- Torstenson & Shaw (2025, *Oikos*, DOI 10.1111/oik.10862) distinguish
  migratory cue accuracy from cue efficacy and show that the fitness value of
  temporal versus environmental cues depends on seasonal context. PAYOFF-B
  therefore must not claim that cue type, cue quality or their fitness
  consequences are new concepts.

- Ketterson et al. (2025, *Journal of Avian Biology*) compare photoperiodic,
  endocrine and gene-expression mechanisms among seasonally sympatric migrant
  and resident populations. PAYOFF-B must not claim novelty for the existence
  of multiple physiological timing architectures.

- Adams et al. (2025, *Ecology*, DOI 10.1002/ecy.70110) show that long-term
  shifts in avian migration phenology do not necessarily compensate for
  changing conditions encountered en route. PAYOFF-B must not claim that a
  shift in calendar timing guarantees ecological compensation.

- A 2025 *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* synthesis, "Reimagining species on the
  move across space and time" (DOI 10.1016/j.tree.2025.03.015), explicitly
  argues that organisms can respond along multiple spatiotemporal dimensions
  simultaneously. PAYOFF-B must not claim the generic value of multi-axis
  climate tracking as new.

These works strengthen the boundary: the candidate novelty is not "multiple
mechanisms", "behavioral flexibility", "different cues", or "compensation".

## Candidate contribution that remains

The specific PAYOFF-B result is narrower and quantitative.

1. Historical final timing precision is represented as a **portfolio** between
   upstream entry precision and downstream correction precision.
2. Two organisms can have the same historical final variance while having
   different hidden feedback shares \(s_1,s_2\).
3. Loss of a fraction of correction opportunity gives exact immediate
   inflation

   \[
   F_i=\exp[(1-\omega_i)s_iP_i].
   \]

4. For equal historical target precision, common opportunity loss gives exact
   hidden-strategy divergence

   \[
   \log(V_1'/V_2')
   =
   (1-\omega)P(s_1-s_2).
   \]

5. A feedback-heavy strategy crosses any fixed variance tolerance after less
   opportunity loss.
6. Under the declared correlated-pair witness, unequal variance inflation adds
   an explicit **asymmetry penalty** to partner mismatch.

The ecological contribution is therefore not hidden variation per se, but:

> **an exact mapping from hidden seasonal clock portfolios to differential
> fragility and interaction mismatch when downstream correction opportunity is
> removed.**

## Safest manuscript wording

> Historically synchronized species can rely on different combinations of
> entry-clock precision and downstream feedback.  Loss of correction
> opportunity can reveal those hidden portfolios as differential timing
> variance and increased interaction mismatch.

## Direct empirical gap

No current PAYOFF-B natural dataset jointly supplies:
- historical final precision;
- independently estimated historical feedback share;
- independently measured opportunity loss;
- post-perturbation final timing variance;
- partner-level covariance on the same phase coordinate.

The theorem therefore remains a prospective mechanistic prediction.
