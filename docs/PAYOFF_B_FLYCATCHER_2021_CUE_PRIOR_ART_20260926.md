# PAYOFF-B 2021 flycatcher cue prior-art boundary

Date: **2026-09-26**

Tomotani et al. (2021; Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution, DOI 10.3389/fevo.2021.630823) is unusually close to the PAYOFF-B information programme and therefore must be treated as prior art rather than rediscovered.

## What that study already established

The study used 11 years of male pied-flycatcher arrival data from the Netherlands and environmental variables from both the Dutch breeding grounds and Ivory Coast wintering grounds.

Published analyses showed that Dutch and Ivory Coast temperature and NDVI explained variation in male arrival timing. The environmental variables that birds appeared to use as arrival cues did not predict the estimated annual optimum arrival date.

The authors explicitly discussed a climate-change explanation: the predictive relationship between cues used for migration timing and the later fitness optimum may have recently deteriorated.

Therefore PAYOFF-B must not claim novelty for:

- migrants using remote wintering-ground information;
- wintering-ground temperature or vegetation predicting arrival timing;
- the idea that climate change can disrupt cue--outcome predictability;
- the distinction between cues used for timing and variables that predict the fitness optimum.

## What PAYOFF-B adds

The candidate contribution remains narrower and downstream:

1. information can arrive after a costly commitment deadline;
2. unequal waiting costs can make partners adopt the same improving cue at different thresholds;
3. this creates a non-monotone desynchronization window even while cue reliability improves monotonically;
4. interaction topology can turn a transient information shock into strict recovery hysteresis.

Tomotani et al. (2021) therefore strengthens the biological motivation for the information axis but raises the novelty bar.

## Data-use boundary

The paper states that raw data will be made available by the authors, but no anonymous raw-data archive was identified in the article page. The study has only 11 years of arrival data, which is below the current 24-year direct-hysteresis readiness threshold for three non-overlapping 8-year information regimes.

Accordingly this source is a prior-art and mechanism anchor, not a direct natural hysteresis test.
