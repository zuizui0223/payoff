# PAYOFF-B clock-portfolio opportunity-loss empirical screen — 2026-10-03

Status: **post-freeze source screen; no natural portfolio-fragility confirmation opened**

## Target prediction

The exact quadratic witness predicts

\[
\frac{V_{\rm disrupted}}{V^*}
=
\exp[(1-\omega)s_{\rm feedback}P].
\]

A direct natural test therefore needs two quantities that are usually absent
from ordinary migration studies:

1. **historical feedback reliance** before the perturbation;
2. **independently measured loss of correction opportunity**.

A simple observation that a disturbed route has greater mismatch is insufficient.

## Tier A — strongest current anchor, not a direct test

### Aikens et al. 2022 industrial-development mule deer

Useful properties:
- same taxon under distinct development regimes;
- long-term GPS data;
- route disturbance is externally defined;
- published development expansion altered movement and reduced green-wave surfing;
- PAYOFF-B reanalysis independently detects lower movement-control permeability in the large-development population.

Missing for the portfolio theorem:
- independent historical s_feedback;
- directly identified omega;
- a common entry-to-final phase-variance target supporting the exact target-relative inflation formula.

Classification: **OPPORTUNITY_LOSS_ANCHOR**.

Do not promote to a portfolio-fragility confirmation.


## Tier A — sudden site-loss natural experiment, but not a timing-variance test

### Pink-footed goose / Filsø restoration

Clausen & Madsen (2016), *Journal of Ornithology* 157:229–237,
DOI 10.1007/s10336-015-1271-9, analysed the sudden 2012 flooding of the former
Filsø agricultural staging area in Denmark.

Useful properties:
- abrupt externally imposed loss of a major staging area;
- individually marked geese with repeated resightings;
- site-faithful return to Filsø fell from about 88.3% before restoration to
  9.3% after restoration;
- former Filsø birds increased use of alternative nearby staging areas,
  especially Ringkøbing Fjord and Limfjorden;
- subsequent spring body condition did not differ detectably from birds that
  had not used Filsø.

This is an important **opportunity-substitution anchor**.

It shows why the portfolio parameter \(\omega\) must not be defined as the
fraction of named sites physically retained.  Loss of one site can be
functionally compensated if alternative correction opportunities remain.

Thus

\[
\text{named-site loss}
\neq
\text{effective opportunity loss}.
\]

For PAYOFF-B, \(\omega\) should represent retained **usable correction
capacity after rerouting/substitution**, not raw habitat count.

Missing for a direct theorem test:
- a historical feedback-precision share \(s_{\rm feedback}\);
- individual entry-phase error on the same coordinate;
- before/after final phase variance or another declared timing-error endpoint.

The published analysis uses site occupancy and body condition, not a
same-coordinate timing-variance outcome.  Although the underlying neck-band
programme contains dated resightings, current public access does not provide a
frozen analysis-ready individual timing table in the PAYOFF-B repository.

Classification: **OPPORTUNITY_SUBSTITUTION_ANCHOR**.

Do not equate the roughly 80–90% decline in Filsø use with
\(1-\omega\).

## Tier A — direct opportunity-loss natural experiment, but no downstream phase

### Piping plover / Hurricane Dorian

Sweeney et al. (2026) examined fall-migrating piping plovers at Ocracoke
Island, North Carolina, before and after Hurricane Dorian (September 2019).

Useful properties:
- abrupt external habitat perturbation;
- remote-sensing habitat change;
- 2016–2023 mark-resighting histories;
- individual identifiers and repeated birds;
- public Dryad data (DOI 10.5061/dryad.0zpc867d2);
- the study reports reduced stopover duration after storm-driven habitat loss
  while body condition did not show the same decline.

This is currently the cleanest **OPPORTUNITY_LOSS_BEHAVIOR_ANCHOR** for the
clock-portfolio framework: environmental disturbance reduced a downstream
migration opportunity and changed stopover use.

Missing for a direct portfolio-fragility test:
- no downstream destination or breeding-arrival phase endpoint;
- no independently reconstructed historical feedback-precision share;
- stopover duration itself is part of the opportunity/behavioral response and
  cannot also be used as the final timing-error endpoint.

Classification: **OPPORTUNITY_LOSS_BEHAVIOR_ANCHOR**.

Do not treat shorter stopover duration as direct phase-error inflation.

## Tier A — selection confound natural experiment

### Great knot / Yellow Sea habitat deterioration

Peng et al. (2023) combine 13 years of great-knot body mass/size data at a
Yellow Sea stopover with resighting-based survival estimates during a period of
habitat loss/deterioration. Late-arriving birds with low fuel stores had the
lowest apparent survival, while population mean arrival advanced and mean fuel
load increased through time. Public data are archived under Dryad
DOI 10.5061/dryad.4xgxd25g8.

This is not a portfolio-fragility confirmation. It is a critical
**SELECTION_REWEIGHTING_ANCHOR** showing that a disturbed population can change
its timing distribution because some migration phenotypes disappear.

Therefore a raw before/after change in population timing mean or variance
cannot be attributed to within-individual clock fragility unless
survival/observation reweighting is handled.

Classification: **SELECTION_REWEIGHTING_ANCHOR**.

## Tier B — historical feedback architecture candidate

### Eurasian curlew long-term GPS tracks

A recent multi-year GPS dataset follows repeated annual cycles of individual
curlews and reports migration timing, number of stopovers, stopover durations,
distances and high repeatability of migration parameters.

Potential use:
- estimate stable individual differences in stopover architecture;
- reconstruct whether individuals consistently use feedback-rich versus feedback-poor routes.

Missing:
- signed seasonal target / resource-wave phase on matched route stages;
- a declared external perturbation that removes opportunity;
- downstream phase-error inflation after that perturbation.

Classification: **HISTORICAL_ARCHITECTURE_CANDIDATE**.

## Tier B — information-route architecture candidate

### Pink-footed goose / Kölzsch et al. 2015

Useful properties:
- multiple route stages;
- stopover regions;
- environmental predictability along migration;
- explicit destination and en-route phenology.

Missing:
- exogenous loss of stopovers or correction opportunities;
- independently estimated historical feedback share.

Classification: **INFORMATION_ROUTE_CANDIDATE**.

## Tier B — one-stopover compensation candidate

### Bar-tailed godwit

Useful properties:
- full-route tracks;
- earlier departure can be absorbed by longer later stopover;
- clear stagewise timing transformation.

Missing:
- variation in number/availability of correction stages;
- exogenous opportunity-loss contrast.

Classification: **STAGEWISE_COMPENSATION_ANCHOR**.

## Direct-test admission rule

A future natural dataset is admitted to a confirmatory portfolio-fragility analysis only if it satisfies all of:

1. same taxon/population or otherwise tightly matched biological architecture;
2. signed entry phase measured before the opportunity perturbation;
3. historical feedback reliance estimated independently of the perturbed downstream outcome;
4. opportunity loss omega defined from habitat/route structure or manipulation before outcome inspection;
5. final phase error or variance measured on the same coordinate;
6. no use of the downstream mismatch outcome to define either s_feedback or omega.

## Preferred future designs

### Within-route natural experiment
Before/after loss of a stopover or barrier opening/closure.

### Matched-route comparison
Same species with historically comparable entry timing but different externally defined numbers of usable stopovers.

### Experimental actuator restriction
Temporarily constrain one correction channel while holding cue information constant.

## Why cross-taxon regression is rejected as primary evidence

A regression such as mismatch ~ migration distance or stopover count mixes physiology, cue reliability, route geometry, body size, energetic cost, breeding ecology and historical selection on feedback.

The clock-portfolio result is therefore kept as an **exact mechanistic prediction awaiting within-system perturbation**, not converted into a weak macroecological correlation.
