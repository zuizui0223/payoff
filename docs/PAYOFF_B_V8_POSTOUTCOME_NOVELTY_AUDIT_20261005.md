# PAYOFF-B V8 post-outcome novelty audit — 2026-10-05

Status: **POSTOUTCOME LITERATURE POSITIONING; DOES NOT ALTER ANY FROZEN RESULT**

## What V8 did not establish as novel

### Temporal change in climatic connectivity is not a first-in-field concept

Saino & Ambrosini (2008, Global Change Biology,
doi:10.1111/j.1365-2486.2007.01488.x) quantified climatic connectivity between
African and European regions relevant to trans-Saharan migrants and related it
to migration timing.

Bauer, McNamara & Barta (2020, Proceedings of the Royal Society B,
doi:10.1098/rspb.2020.0622) explicitly argued that route-scale environmental
predictability should be quantified and that repeating climatic-connectivity
analyses across past decades could test whether correlations have changed and
whether this matters for migrants.

Therefore PAYOFF-B must not claim priority for asking whether cross-site
predictive connectivity changes through time.

### Increasing spatial synchrony of spring phenology is not itself novel

Liu et al. (2019, Geophysical Research Letters,
doi:10.1029/2018GL081370) showed that climatic warming can increase spatial
synchrony in spring vegetation phenology across the Northern Hemisphere.

Therefore the positive V8 delta-rho is not by itself a new general climate
phenomenon. It is important here because it supplies an observed environmental
change on the same source-target mappings used for the bird-transfer test.

### Environmental synchrony need not map identically onto migrant phenology

Vriend et al. (2023, Ecology, doi:10.1002/ecy.3908) found that temperature
strongly influenced spatial synchrony in laying date of resident blue and great
tits but not migratory pied flycatchers.

This is close prior art for the broader idea that synchrony in environmental
conditions need not translate uniformly into synchrony in migrant biological
timing. PAYOFF-B should therefore avoid a generic priority claim that
environmental synchrony and migratory timing can decouple.

## What remains distinctive in the current evidence stack

The potentially distinctive contribution is the combination of three layers
within one explicit prediction-control framework:

1. **Longitudinal route-environment change:** on the frozen Amaral
   source-target mappings, predictive green-up connectivity increased strongly
   and robustly between 2002–2009 and 2010–2017.

2. **Matched biological realization test:** using the same mappings and a
   prospectively locked downstream analysis, the increase did not correspond to
   a detectable aggregate improvement in arrival-green-up matching, and larger
   connectivity gains did not predict larger matching gains.

3. **Mechanistic separation of information and recourse:** the PAYOFF-B theory
   and individual-level movement evidence distinguish information available
   before/along migration from the downstream actions that can still alter
   arrival phase.

The novelty is therefore not:
- that environmental predictability matters;
- that climatic connectivity changes;
- that warming can synchronize vegetation phenology;
- or that migrant phenology can differ from resident phenology.

The strongest defensible novelty proposition is:

> **Seasonal adaptation should be evaluated as a conversion problem, not only
> an information problem: changes in environmental predictability and changes
> in biological matching can be empirically separated, and improved
> predictability need not yield detectable improvement in matching.**

## Important limitation

The V8 transfer coefficient is not significantly different from zero under the
locked two-way cluster-robust inference. Its point estimate is positive, but the
95% interval includes zero.

Accordingly, PAYOFF-B cannot claim that increasing predictability *causes*
worsening mismatch or that migrants systematically fail to use newly available
information.

The supported empirical statement is weaker and cleaner:

> Predictive connectivity increased strongly, while phenological matching did
> not detectably improve; across the eligible mappings, greater increases in
> connectivity were not associated with greater improvements in matching.

## Journal consequence

The original V8 escalation route based on a newly demonstrated broad
degradation of environmental information is closed.

The current evidence stack remains especially well aligned with a conceptual
and mechanistic journal because the strongest contribution is the separation
of prediction from actionability/recourse.

Current targeting recommendation:
1. **The American Naturalist** — strongest fit for the theory-first integrated
   paper.
2. **Ecology Letters** — defensible stretch if the paper is rebuilt around the
   general prediction-versus-actionability principle and the V8
   environment-to-biology conversion test is presented as a decisive
   falsification of the simpler information-only expectation.
3. **Global Ecology and Biogeography** — strong empirical fallback if the
   macroecological route analysis is foregrounded.
4. **PNAS / Nature Ecology & Evolution** — not justified by the present public
   reanalysis alone; a stronger independent dataset or direct broad-scale
   measurement of actionability/recourse would be needed for a credible top
   route.

No journal ranking in this document changes the frozen statistical claims.
