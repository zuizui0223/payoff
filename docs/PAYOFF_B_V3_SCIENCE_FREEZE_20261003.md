## Frozen title

**A seasonal timer–controller architecture can convert shared environmental
change into phenological mismatch**

## Frozen ecological conclusion

Shared environmental change need not produce shared timing. A
developmental/physiological entry timer determines the phase error with which
an organism enters a seasonal trajectory. An information-dependent downstream
controller then determines whether that error is retained, corrected or
amplified. Differences between interacting actors can therefore convert a
shared environmental displacement into phenological mismatch even when the
actors begin with the same phase error.

## Frozen exact spine

1. Information quality can improve while retained actionability declines.
2. The entry timer determines entry timing and the initial phase state.
3. The downstream controller uses phase information and remaining actuator
   opportunity to transform that state.
4. Pairwise mismatch decomposes exactly as

   [
   \Delta_n
   =
   (\lambda_1^n-\lambda_2^n)m_0
   +
   \frac{\lambda_1^n+\lambda_2^n}{2}\Delta_0.
   ]

5. The first term is controller-generated mismatch; the second is
   timer-propagated mismatch.
6. Physical/actionability loss and strategic coordination are separate reasons
   that mismatch may fail to recover.

## Actionability–opportunity stitch

The reduced actionability function `r(t)` and the downstream opportunity
factor `O_t` are linked but are not declared universally identical.

- `r(t)` summarizes the fraction of the full state-contingent response that
  remains usable at a route stage or decision time.
- `O_t` is the actuator-specific opportunity factor entering
  `h_t = O_t g_t` in a particular phase-control channel.
- They coincide only in the scalar one-opportunity reduction.

No natural `r`, `O`, `g`, `K`, `phi` or `D_eff` value may be
back-solved from the same response used to support the mechanism.

## Natural-evidence ceiling

Current natural evidence provides layered support, not an end-to-end natural
identification of the complete serial mechanism.

- broad birds: pooled directional predictive-connectivity association;
- mule deer: strongest same-population timer–controller anchor, including
  continuous phase convergence and signed downstream actuator associations;
- godwit, goose, redstart and flycatcher systems: stagewise information,
  buffering, recourse and interaction anchors;
- registered negative results remain negative and are not reinterpreted as
  support.

The mule-deer evidence does not identify the full controller, prove causal
independence of entry and downstream mechanisms, or prospectively validate the
serial decomposition.
