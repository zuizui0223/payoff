# PAYOFF-B two-clock natural-evidence matrix — 2026-10-03

Status: **post-freeze synthesis; frozen GEB V2 unchanged**

## Purpose

The two-clock architecture is not a taxonomic classification.  It separates
two mechanisms:

1. developmental/physiological readiness \(G\);
2. information-dependent decision control \(K,g\).

Natural systems differ in which layer is currently observable.

## Evidence matrix

| system | developmental/readiness evidence | decision-control evidence | current PAYOFF-B role | claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| insect diapause / bee emergence | strong general prior literature for photoperiodic, thermal, endocrine and sometimes circadian-clock control of diapause/emergence | not identified for the emergence event in current PAYOFF-B data | developmental-timer anchor | do not call all bee emergence one molecular oscillator |
| Red Desert mule deer | no independent physiological readiness variable for the focal start-to-end phase transition | strong signed movement-rate and stopover response; continuous phase funnel in 152 animal-years | strongest current decision-controller natural anchor | cannot separate \(G\) from \(g\), or estimate \(K,g,\phi,Q\) jointly |
| long-distance migratory birds | established endogenous/circannual and photoperiodic contribution to migratory readiness in prior literature | stopover departure, route pacing and en-route adjustment are established decision stages | strongest conceptual hybrid class | current PAYOFF-B broad-bird data do not jointly identify both layers within individuals |
| bar-tailed godwit | endogenous readiness not independently quantified in PAYOFF-B | early departure absorbed by later stopover extension | signed/stagewise recourse anchor | does not identify internal phase estimate or readiness gate |
| pink-footed goose | readiness programme not independently quantified | cue relevance changes along route stages | en-route information-updating anchor | does not estimate \(G,K,g\) jointly |
| Eurasian wigeon | no independent readiness decomposition | preregistered phase-retention/correction analyses; predictive-connectivity amplification not supported | negative boundary separating prediction from correction | null cannot be reinterpreted as confirmation of two-clock theory |
| flowering plants | strong developmental/physiological timing and threshold-like commitment plausible/established broadly | little post-flowering recourse for the focal event; no route-like controller assumed | low-recourse developmental-clock contrast | taxon identity is not a readiness parameter |
| local plant–pollinator interactions | partner-specific temperature sensitivities and timing shifts | direct signed within-individual feedback usually not measured | interaction response-asymmetry anchor | cannot infer \(G,K,g\) from partner phenology slopes alone |

## Key conclusion

No current natural PAYOFF-B system jointly identifies all of

\[
G,\quad K,\quad g,\quad \phi,\quad Q
\]

for the same focal transition.

The evidence is complementary rather than complete:

\[
\text{developmental systems}
\Rightarrow
\text{readiness/timer mechanism}
\]

and

\[
\text{movement systems}
\Rightarrow
\text{state-dependent correction mechanism}.
\]

The direct two-clock decomposition is therefore prospective.

## Strongest same-system target

A migratory system is the best direct target because it can expose both layers
within one individual:

1. quantify endogenous/physiological readiness before migration or a focal
   stopover departure;
2. quantify checkpoint phase information;
3. observe the subsequent decision;
4. observe downstream phase;
5. repeat across stages.

A useful design would combine physiological markers or experimentally defined
readiness with GPS/accelerometry and environmental phase estimates.

## Strongest cross-system prediction

Phenological sensitivity alone should not classify vulnerability.

The same apparent temperature slope can arise from:
- a flexible developmental timer with weak downstream recourse;
- a rigid timer with strong decision feedback;
- a hybrid with both.

Therefore a more mechanistic comparative space is

\[
\boxed{
\text{timer plasticity}
\times
\text{decision-feedback recourse}.
}
\]

This yields four architectures:

| timer plasticity | feedback recourse | expected behavior |
|---|---|---|
| low | low | mismatch accumulates and is hard to rescue |
| high | low | good initial tracking, little correction after commitment |
| low | high | poor initial tracking can be repaired downstream |
| high | high | both anticipatory and corrective tracking |

This classification is a theory prediction, not a completed cross-taxon
empirical ranking.

## Falsifiable consequence

If two taxa have similar final timing but occupy different clock architectures,
their trajectories should differ.

A timer-dominated taxon should show most correction before the focal event.
A decision-controller-dominated taxon should show signed error-dependent
adjustment after movement or action begins.

Thus **trajectory shape**, not only final phenology, is the relevant empirical
discriminator.
