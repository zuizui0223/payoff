## Absolute recruitment and best observed option

The source assigns 50 host eggs per vial. E is emerged parasitoids divided by initially supplied host eggs, averaged at the vial/cell level, a realized absolute emergence measure (not conditional infection success, demographic growth rate, or lifetime fitness). Three females were introduced in parasitoid vials in the original experiment. Original author normalized parasitism success and control-minus-exposed host emergence are also retained, with explicit control definitions; these are distinct response metrics. Vials are replicates, not individual eggs. No confidence intervals, independent population replication, or verified replicate-incubator inference claimed for this descriptive calculation.

These point envelopes are the best observed mean among the seven single-host assays in each environment; selection is in-sample and not a biological upper bound or prospective choice accuracy. No observations of behavioral host switching, simultaneous host availability, resource timing manipulation, or actual switching costs.

The substantive connection to wave 16 is distinction between a resource's availability, information about its quality, and absolute performance even under hypothetical perfect selection. Lue et al. already reported temperature-dependent reductions in parasitism success and realized diet breadth; the new arithmetic is a direct option-capacity diagnostic, not independent discovery of thermal collapse. The result should NOT be promoted to the original primary theta, since stage-appropriate exposure schedules were used, not experimentally crossed phenological delays.
