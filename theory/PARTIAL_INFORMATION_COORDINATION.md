# PAYOFF-B partial-information coordination game

Status: **exploratory theoretical extension, 2026-09-26**

## Ecological question

The existing PAYOFF-B tracking model assumes that interacting lineages respond
to a moving environmental demand that is available to the model at the time of
response. That is a strong information assumption.

A resident plant or local pollinator may read destination spring conditions
directly. A long-distance migrant may have to commit to departure before the
destination state is observable. Its action is therefore chosen from a remote
cue rather than from the realized future state.

The new question is:

> Can a viable coordinated timing response exist, while a migrant rationally
> declines to make that response because its pre-departure information is not
> strong enough?

This is a Bayesian coordination problem, not a quantum-mechanical claim.

## Minimal hidden-state game

Let the destination state be

    theta in {0,1},

where 1 denotes an early spring. The prior probability of an early spring is

    pi = P(theta=1).

The destination-resident guild observes theta before acting and matches it.
The migrant acts earlier and sees a binary remote cue s with symmetric accuracy

    q = P(s=theta),  q >= 1/2.

The migrant chooses

    a in {late, early}.

Let:

    C_F = private cost of false early departure,
    C_M = private cost of missing an early spring,
    I_M = migrant's own interaction-mismatch cost,
    I_R = mismatch cost imposed on each resident partner,
    n_R = number of resident partners.

Because residents match theta, migrant--resident interaction mismatch occurs
exactly when the migrant chooses the wrong state-contingent timing.

## Exact private decision threshold

Let

    p = P(theta=1 | s)

be the migrant's posterior probability of an early spring.

Expected private loss from choosing early is

    (1-p)(C_F + I_M).

Expected private loss from choosing late is

    p(C_M + I_M).

Therefore early departure is privately optimal iff

    p > tau_private,

where

    tau_private
    = (C_F + I_M)
      / (C_F + C_M + 2 I_M).

When false early departure is more costly than missing an early spring,
tau_private > 1/2.

## Exact joint-payoff threshold

The same mismatch also harms the resident partners. Joint loss adds

    n_R I_R

to either kind of timing error.

The joint-optimal threshold is therefore

    tau_joint
    = (C_F + I_M + n_R I_R)
      / (C_F + C_M + 2 I_M + 2 n_R I_R).

If

    C_F > C_M
    and
    n_R I_R > 0,

then

    tau_joint < tau_private.

Thus there is a posterior interval

    tau_joint < p <= tau_private

in which advancing is optimal for the interacting system but not for the
migrant acting on its private payoff.

We call this the **information--coordination wedge**.

## Cue-reliability threshold

For an early remote cue under a symmetric cue model,

    p_1
    = pi q / [pi q + (1-pi)(1-q)].

For any action threshold tau, the cue accuracy at which p_1=tau is

    q*(tau)
    = tau(1-pi)
      / [pi(1-tau) + tau(1-pi)].

Hence the wedge can also be expressed directly on cue reliability:

    q*_joint < q <= q*_private.

As cross-site predictability declines through this interval, the same early cue
still contains enough information to justify early timing at the joint-system
level, but no longer enough to justify that timing privately for the migrant.

## Canonical numerical witness

The implementation uses the following transparent witness:

    pi  = 0.55
    C_F = 2.0
    C_M = 1.0
    I_M = 0.5
    I_R = 1.0
    n_R = 2.

Then

    tau_private = 0.625
    tau_joint   = 0.5625,

and

    q*_private ~= 0.576923
    q*_joint   ~= 0.512658.

At

    q = 0.55,

an early cue gives

    P(theta=1 | early cue) ~= 0.59901.

Therefore

    joint system: early is optimal,
    migrant privately: late is optimal.

The disagreement is not caused by insufficient phenological capacity: both
actions are available. It is generated by partial information plus an
interaction externality.

## Deficit decomposition

The minimal model holds capacity fixed as adequate.

Let:

    L_PI = joint loss with perfect information,
    L_C  = minimum expected joint loss using the available cue,
    L_D  = expected joint loss under the migrant's decentralized private policy.

Here L_PI=0. Define

    capacity deficit    = 0,
    information deficit = L_C - L_PI,
    coordination deficit = L_D - L_C.

Then

    total adaptation deficit
    = capacity deficit
      + information deficit
      + coordination deficit

exactly.

This gives PAYOFF-B a direct way to distinguish three conceptually different
reasons why a viable adaptive response may not be realized:

1. the response is physically unavailable;
2. the response is not identifiable from information available at commitment;
3. the response is identifiable as jointly valuable but privately inaccessible.

## Connection to the existing coordination barrier

The existing moving-landscape result shows

    coordinated joint gain > 0
    while
    unilateral gain A < 0
    and
    unilateral gain B < 0.

That is a strategic accessibility barrier under a known environmental demand.

The present extension is different. It adds uncertainty before commitment.
The intended combined PAYOFF-B sequence is:

    finite adaptive capacity
    x partial information
    x partner dependence
    ->
    information-limited decisions
    + information--coordination wedge
    + classical coordination barrier
    ->
    realized mismatch / persistence.

The two mechanisms should remain separately identifiable in code and figures.

## Ecological prediction

The extension predicts that mismatch should depend not only on the magnitude of
destination warming but on the reliability of information linking the
decision site to the destination state.

A direct empirical test would compare systems or years with similar destination
spring advance but different pre-departure cue predictability. Long-distance
migrants should show the largest timing deficit where destination-state
predictability from departure-site cues has weakened.

This is a prospective prediction. The current repository does not yet contain
a source-backed estimate showing that climate change has reduced a particular
migration teleconnection.


## Prior-art boundary

The broad ecological ingredients are not claimed as new.

Phenological mismatch in long-distance migrants, the need to anticipate future
conditions at distant destinations, and the importance of environmental
connectivity between departure and destination sites are established ideas in
migration ecology. Work on Asian houbara, for example, provides direct evidence
that migrants can use departure-site temperature when that cue is correlated
with later conditions along the migration system. The phrase and general idea
of climate-driven **informational mismatch** in interspecific interactions also
predate this model.

Accordingly, PAYOFF-B must not claim novelty for:

- the existence of phenological mismatch;
- the statement that long-distance migrants have incomplete information about
  destination conditions;
- the general idea that climate change can alter informational relationships.

The candidate contribution here is narrower and mathematical:

1. derive separate private and joint posterior thresholds under the same
   available cue;
2. show an exact information--coordination wedge produced by interaction
   externalities;
3. decompose adaptation deficit into information and decentralized-strategy
   components on a common payoff scale;
4. connect that Bayesian pre-commitment wedge to the already independent
   PAYOFF-B unilateral coordination barrier after the environmental state is
   known.

This prior-art boundary should remain explicit if the extension is promoted.

## Claim boundary

This model does not establish that:

- any named bird species currently occupies the canonical parameter values;
- climate change has empirically weakened a particular remote cue correlation;
- plants or pollinators always have perfect local information;
- migrants optimize expected payoff exactly;
- the binary early/late action space is biologically complete.

The current contribution is an exact mechanism result and a testable empirical
prediction.
