# Wave 20 — movement as information acquisition

Theoretical extension of Wave 19; no biological data or new primary theta. A two-stage finite decision problem with binary hidden destination spring H, equal prior, terminal matching reward 1 and mismatch reward 0. At stage zero choose a commitment mode: direct (commit immediately), mobile (move to a checkpoint then observe), or wait (remain at origin then observe). At stage one choose either terminal timing action after the signal. Both terminal actions remain feasible for mobile and wait in this minimal witness. The direct action has no signal and no cost. Mobile and wait receive symmetric binary signals of accuracy q_m and q_w in [0.5,1], respectively, and pay nonnegative total costs c_m,c_w regardless of terminal action. Costs include travel or delay penalties in common reward units; these are assumptions, not measured bird costs.

Bellman recursion: V_t(x,b)=max_a { -c_t(x,a)+E[V_(t+1)(x',b')|x,b,a] }, with posterior b' obtained from the action-dependent transition/observation kernel. Terminal value is max over feasible timing choices of posterior expected performance. This recursion assumes the state includes all relevant physical constraints and information, and finite horizon. The witness is an exact special case:
V_direct=0.5; V_mobile=q_m-c_m; V_wait=q_w-c_w.
Mobile strictly wins iff q_m-c_m>max(0.5,q_w-c_w). Thus movement may be selected for information even when it has no direct matching benefit before the checkpoint. With q_m>q_w, a positive excess mobile cost can be justified up to q_m-q_w, provided the direct option is also beaten. Conversely if q_m=q_w, the cheaper information mode weakly dominates. This is a decision-theoretic witness, not a novelty claim about active sensing or a game equilibrium.

Example q_m=.9, q_w=.75, c_w=.15: wait=.60, direct=.50; mobile wins for c_m<.30, ties at .30. At c_m=.10, mobile=.80. At c_m=.40, wait wins. Information acquisition and remaining actionability are not inherently opposed: action can improve future observation while preserving choice. If movement instead irreversibly fixes timing before observation, the signal cannot improve the terminal choice and mobile value is .5-c_m. This is a distinct feasible-set counterfactual, not a fitted mediation estimate.

Empirical falsifier: compare predecision information and independently defined downstream feasible options at matched stages, with held-out years/animals and separately observed resource-relative target. Do not infer cue perception from retrospective weather, or information acquisition from movement alone. Wave 19 fixed-commitment decomposition can be evaluated conditional on each mode; differences across optimized modes are not invariant additive causal attribution. Strategic interactions require a separate game.

Validation: exhaustive synthetic grid of 21^3=9261 combinations for q_m,q_w,c_m, c_w=.15; checked optimal value dominates all three available modes.
