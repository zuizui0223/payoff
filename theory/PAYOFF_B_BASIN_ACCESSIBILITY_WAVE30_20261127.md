## Exact local stability
Each species has four haplotypes 00,10,01,11 and relative log payoff vector g_i=(0,-.06,-D*r_i^2-.06*r_i,g11_i). Under coupling G=J*r_A*r_B, the 11 log payoff receives G*h_j, where h_j is the partner 11 frequency. The simultaneous deterministic map is viability selection followed by recombination with R_i. No mutation or sampling. At a monomorphic 00/11 corner of both populations, let gh_i=g11_i+G*h_j. For an ancestral focal corner, the three rare-haplotype eigenvalues are exp(g10_i), exp(g01_i), (1-R_i)*exp(gh_i). For a fully adapted focal corner, they are exp(g10_i-gh_i), exp(g01_i-gh_i), (1-R_i)*exp(-gh_i). Cross-species derivatives vanish at monomorphic corners. The six eigenvalues classify local stability when none have modulus one. Numerical one-sided Jacobians match analytic eigenvalues across all 96 corners to maximum absolute error 1.225e-8. This does not establish global stability or exclude interior equilibria.

Rare-double invasion conditional on near-ancestral focal population requires h_j>-[g11_i+log(1-R_i)]/G when G>0. This is not the finite-frequency threshold: once both alleles are common, recombination can reassemble the double mutant and mean fitness changes. In the symmetric qmax=.9,R=.04 coupled witness, s_i=-.02375532785 and G=.03288177778, so conditional rare-invasion threshold is .72244658. Ancestral-ancestral and adapted-adapted corners are locally attracting, whereas mixed monomorphic corners each have an invading mode >1. The uncoupled control has all four corners locally attracting. Exact absorbing boundaries remain even when linearly unstable.

## Executed finite-frequency experiment
24 settings, 13,488 deterministic trajectories, 4,000 generations. Each population has equal initial marginal derived frequencies p_i at its two loci. Coupling starts (1-p,0,0,p), 21 grid points 0..1; repulsion starts (1-2p,p,p,0), 11 grid points 0..0.5. Compare the 121 matched initial pairs on common support [0,.5]^2. Endpoints are categorized by both populations having ancestral or double haplotype >=.99, or unresolved. This is a finite-horizon classification, not a certified basin. Across all settings: 44 unresolved endpoints, 418 one-step residuals >1e-9, including 374 already in a corner category. Preserve these rather than rounding to equilibrium. No proof of a unique continuous separatrix.

At qB=.9, RA=RB=.04: all endpoints have residual <=1e-9 and no unresolved. On the 121 matched initial pairs:
| positive coupling between species | initial genetic coupling: both 11 | initial genetic repulsion: both 11 |
|---|---:|---:|
| absent (G=0) | 36/121 | 4/121 |
| present | 85/121 | 36/121 |
With G present, 49 matched pairs reach 11-11 only with initial genetic coupling, none only with repulsion, 36 with either; endpoints differ (all categories) for 57 pairs. Along p_A=p_B, minimum grid hit in coupled selection is .20 (previous .15 fails) in initial genetic coupling, .45 (previous .40 fails) in repulsion. Without positive cross-term, respective minima .25 and .45. These are grid brackets, not continuous critical frequencies. Both seed frequencies can be far below the local .722 rare-mutant gate and still reach the adapted corner. In repulsion p=.45 means each locus has derived allele frequency .45, but initial 11 frequency is zero; recombination creates 11. Do not equate p and 11 frequency.

Low-q contrast: qB=.8 never reaches both adapted from matched p_i<=.5 in any of eight settings (four R pairs x presence/absence G). Yet all four coupled high-high corners are locally attracting, and some larger coupling initial frequencies reach high-high. Thus inability of rare invasion, finite-frequency accessibility, and local stability are distinct. qB=.85 with RA=.005,RB=.02 gives 97 vs 16/121 matched endpoints for coupling vs repulsion under G, but 52 repulsion trajectories fail residual criterion; treat only as finite-horizon descriptive result. No sample prevalence, ecological frequency, or significance claim. Parameter grid deliberately constructed.
