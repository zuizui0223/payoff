# Wave 21 — private information and coordination

Binary state H with equal prior; two species simultaneously choose binary timing actions after conditionally independent symmetric private signals of accuracy q_i >= 1/2. Payoff u_i=1{a_i=H}+k 1{a_i=a_j}, k>=0. This is a common-interest coordination component with separate individual environmental matching. Four deterministic signal policies: constant 0, follow, invert, constant 1. No movement cost in this stage; q_i can be interpreted as induced by a previously fixed mode, not an endogenous acquisition equilibrium.

When both follow, mismatch probability m=q1+q2-2q1q2. Conditional on own signal, the gain from following rather than flipping is (2q_i-1)[1+k(2q_j-1)] >=0. Hence follow/follow is a Bayesian Nash equilibrium, strict for q_i>1/2. Increasing q1 weakly decreases mismatch in this equilibrium when q2>=1/2. Do not claim that better independent symmetric information intrinsically disrupts synchrony.

When the partner always chooses 0, a species with signal 1 gains 2q_i-1-k by deviating from 0 to 1. Thus constant-0/constant-0 (and symmetric constant-1 pair) is an equilibrium iff max_i(2q_i-1)<=k. Better information can destroy a constant coordination equilibrium at q_i>(1+k)/2. This is an equilibrium-set change, not a unique predicted transition. If species 2 remains constant 0 and species 1 follows, species 2 prefers constant 0 iff (2q2-1)[1+k(2q1-1)]<=0 for its signal 1; therefore this asymmetric pure equilibrium is impossible for q2>1/2. At q2=1/2 it may exist only if the coordination benefit of following the other species is zero conditional on its own uninformative signal (true in this symmetric model). The follow/constant configuration requires 2q1-1>=k.

Witness q2=.6, k=.5: at q1=.6 constant pairs and follow/follow coexist; at q1=.9 only follow/follow among deterministic policies. A transition from constant coordination to follow/follow increases mismatch from 0 to .42, while mean environmental matching increases from .5 to .75. Per-species expected payoff changes from 1 to 1.19 (species 1) and .89 (species 2). Total payoff increases from 2 to 2.08. The less-informed partner loses even though total payoff rises. No equilibrium selection or evolutionary dynamics specified; mixed equilibria not enumerated. A constant pair cannot persist as equilibrium at q1=.9. The comparison is conditional on starting in a constant equilibrium and ending in follow/follow.

General welfare difference between follow/follow and constant pair = q1+q2-1-2km. Partner i loses iff q_i-.5<km. These are exact conditional comparisons, not a claim that information necessarily harms welfare. Prior-art novelty not audited.

Validation: exhaustive 4x4 deterministic policy enumeration on 21^3=9261 synthetic parameter combinations; at least one pure equilibrium in each. No real biological fit, no CI. Empirical prediction requires independent estimates of signal quality, timing matching, and interaction benefits; no claim about observed cue use. Pardikes primary source HOLD unchanged.
