# PAYOFF-B prospective phase-variance funnel theorem

Date: **2026-10-03**  
Status: **prospective post-freeze Paper-2 extension; frozen GEB V2 unchanged**

## Compatibility with the two-clock architecture

This theorem was first written for a single enacted feedback gain \(g\). After
the explicit two-clock split, its \(g\) should be interpreted as **effective
correction**

\[
h=Gg_{\mathrm{decision}},
\]

unless full readiness \(G=1\) is independently known.

Accordingly, the mean/variance inverse in this file identifies information
weight \(K\) and effective correction \(h\). It does not separate
physiological readiness from decision gain without an additional measurement
or intervention.

## 1. Why variance matters

The route-wise controller already predicts how the **mean signed phase error**
changes across checkpoints. A stronger test of individual feedback is whether
the **between-individual spread of phase error** also contracts.

This distinguishes two mechanisms that can produce similar mean timing.

### Open-loop timing programme

All individuals receive the same correction \(u_t\), independent of their own
incoming phase error. A common shift can change the population mean but does
not selectively pull early and late individuals toward one another.

### Individual feedback

Each individual receives a noisy checkpoint cue about its own phase state and
changes speed, stopover duration or route according to its estimated signed
error. Early and late individuals then receive corrections in opposite
directions, which can reduce between-individual phase variance.

The ecological signature is a **phase-variance funnel**:

> wide phase dispersion before a checkpoint, narrower dispersion after
> informative state-dependent correction.

Synchronization itself is not a new ecological phenomenon. The contribution
here is the explicit reduced-model distinction between common open-loop timing
and individualized phase feedback.

## 2. Gaussian checkpoint model

Let incoming signed phase error across individuals be

\[
E_t\sim N(m_t,P_t).
\]

Each individual receives

\[
Z_t=E_t+\nu_t,
\qquad
\nu_t\sim N(0,R_t).
\]

The scalar posterior weight is

\[
K_t=\frac{P_t}{P_t+R_t}.
\]

The posterior error variance is

\[
P_t^+=(1-K_t)P_t,
\]

while the across-individual variance of posterior means is

\[
\operatorname{Var}\{\hat E_t\}=K_tP_t.
\]

Let the individualized controller use

\[
u_t=g_t\hat E_t.
\]

Before passive route propagation,

\[
E_t-u_t
=
(1-g_t)\hat E_t+\xi_t,
\]

where \(\xi_t=E_t-\hat E_t\) is the posterior estimation error.

Therefore the unconditional residual variance is exactly

\[
\boxed{
P_{t,\mathrm{res}}
=
P_t\left[1-K_tg_t(2-g_t)\right].
}
\]

After passive retention \(\phi_t\) and independent process innovation with
variance \(Q_t\),

\[
\boxed{
P_{t+1}
=
\phi_t^2P_t
\left[1-K_tg_t(2-g_t)\right]
+
Q_t.
}
\]

## 3. Feedback fingerprint against an open-loop schedule

If all individuals receive a common correction independent of their own phase,
subtracting that same value changes the mean but not the variance. The
open-loop variance is therefore

\[
P_{t+1}^{\mathrm{open}}
=
\phi_t^2P_t+Q_t.
\]

The extra variance reduction generated by individualized feedback is

\[
\boxed{
P_{t+1}^{\mathrm{open}}
-
P_{t+1}^{\mathrm{feedback}}
=
\phi_t^2P_tK_tg_t(2-g_t).
}
\]

For

\[
0<g_t<2
\]

and informative cues

\[
K_t>0,
\]

this difference is strictly positive.

Thus an individualized feedback controller predicts variance contraction
beyond what can be produced by applying the same timing shift to every
individual.

This does **not** prove cognition or explicit Bayesian calculation. It is a
functional statistical signature of state-dependent individualized correction.

## 4. Noisy information affects both mean retention and the variance funnel

Under perfect phase observation \(K_t=1\),

\[
\lambda_t=\phi_t(1-g_t)
\]

and

\[
P_{t+1}
=
\lambda_t^2P_t+Q_t.
\]

With noisy information, however, the correction is based on a posterior
estimate rather than the true incoming phase. The regression of outgoing on
incoming phase becomes

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_t
=
\phi_t(1-g_tK_t).
}
\]

The population variance still obeys

\[
P_{t+1}
=
\phi_t^2P_t[1-K_tg_t(2-g_t)]+Q_t.
\]

Thus cue quality enters **both** observable signatures. The
perfect-information identity \(\lambda=\phi(1-g)\) is not licensed when
\(K<1\).

Two routes can nevertheless have the same observed mean \(\lambda\) and
different variance funnels because the product \(gK\) can be the same while
\(g\) and \(K\) differ. Mean retention alone therefore cannot identify whether
weak phase carry-over reflects accurate information with modest correction or
noisier information with stronger correction.

This supplies two observable axes:

1. **mean retention** — the regression-scale persistence of signed phase error;
2. **variance retention** — the population spread remaining after
   individualized information and correction.

## 5. Mean + variance retention can identify information weight and effective feedback

Define

\[
d_t
=
1-\frac{\lambda_t}{\phi_t}
=
g_tK_t
\]

and the innovation-adjusted normalized variance retention

\[
v_t
=
\frac{P_{t+1}-Q_t}
{\phi_t^2P_t}.
\]

The variance recursion gives

\[
v_t
=
1-2d_t+\frac{d_t^2}{K_t}.
\]

Therefore, whenever \(d_t\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
K_t
=
\frac{d_t^2}
{v_t-1+2d_t}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
g_t
=
\frac{d_t}{K_t}.
}
\]

Under the scalar Gaussian observation model,

\[
R_t
=
P_t\frac{1-K_t}{K_t}.
\]

This creates a stronger prospective **phase-sense inverse**: if passive
retention \(\phi_t\), process innovation \(Q_t\), mean phase retention
\(\lambda_t\), and incoming/outgoing phase variances are independently
identified, the declared Gaussian model separates an effective checkpoint
information weight \(K_t\) from a feedback gain \(g_t\).

The inverse is not identified when \(\lambda_t=\phi_t\), because only the
product \(g_tK_t=0\) is then observed. More importantly, one may not estimate
\(\phi\) or \(Q\) from the same closed-loop transition and then claim that the
resulting \(K\) and \(g\) are independent biological estimates. A valid design
needs an external or experimental passive-retention reference, an actuator
contrast, or another source of identification.

This inverse links the animal's functional "phase sense" to observable
population moments without claiming that the animal neurally computes a
Bayesian weight.

## 6. Deadbeat correction does not remove uncertainty when cues are noisy

At \(g_t=1\),

\[
P_{t+1}
=
\phi_t^2(1-K_t)P_t+Q_t.
\]

Even a controller that fully corrects the **estimated** phase error leaves
residual dispersion if the phase estimate itself is uncertain.

Only when \(K_t=1\) does incoming variance disappear completely, leaving only
new process innovation \(Q_t\).

This is the population-level form of Schrödinger's spring: perfect willingness
to correct cannot compensate for information the animal does not yet possess.

## 7. Overshoot boundary

Because

\[
g(2-g)
\]

is positive only for \(0<g<2\),

- \(0<g<2\): informative individualized feedback contracts variance relative
  to the open-loop baseline;
- \(g=2\): no net variance advantage over open loop in the perfect linear
  representation;
- \(g>2\): overcorrection amplifies between-individual phase variance.

Thus very strong gain is not automatically better.

## 8. Homogeneous stationary variance

For constant \(R,Q,\phi,g\),

\[
P_{t+1}
=
\phi^2
\left[
P_t-
g(2-g)\frac{P_t^2}{P_t+R}
\right]+Q.
\]

When

\[
|\phi(1-g)|<1,
\]

a finite non-negative fixed point solves

\[
A P_*^2+B P_*-QR=0,
\]

with

\[
A=1-\phi^2(1-g)^2
\]

and

\[
B=R(1-\phi^2)-Q.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
P_*=
\frac{-B+\sqrt{B^2+4AQR}}{2A}.
}
\]

This gives the irreducible phase-dispersion floor generated jointly by noisy
checkpoint information and new environmental innovation.

## 9. Direct ecological predictions

### Prediction 1 — variance funnel

Across comparable individuals, signed phase-error variance should decline
across informative route checkpoints more than expected from passive carry-over
alone.

### Prediction 2 — cue-quality dependence

Holding actuator architecture comparable, checkpoint stages with more
informative local cues should show stronger downstream variance contraction for
\(0<g<2\).

### Prediction 3 — actuator removal

If speed, stopover or route flexibility is experimentally or anthropogenically
reduced while cue quality is unchanged, mean phase retention and/or downstream
phase variance should increase.

### Prediction 4 — same mean lambda, different spread

Routes with similar mean \(\lambda\) can differ in downstream phase variance.
Such a contrast would be evidence that mean retention alone is insufficient to
characterize the controller.

### Prediction 5 — open-loop versus feedback

A fixed circannual or photoperiodic programme can shift mean timing without
requiring individualized variance contraction. A phase-error-dependent
controller predicts both signed actuator responses and an additional narrowing
of the downstream phase distribution.

This is the clearest functional test of the proposed "Mikawa-Anjo clock."

## 10. Current evidence boundary

The existing natural anchors show compensation, route-stage updating and phase
retention, but the repository does not currently contain an independent
individual-level test of the full variance-funnel prediction.

Published work already documents synchronization and compensation in migratory
systems, so PAYOFF-B should not claim the ecological phenomenon of convergence
itself as new.

The prospective contribution is the joint test:

\[
\text{incoming individual phase}
\rightarrow
\text{individual checkpoint information}
\rightarrow
\text{signed correction}
\rightarrow
\text{mean retention + variance contraction}.
\]

A direct test should compare a common-schedule model against an individualized
feedback model on held-out downstream phase data.
