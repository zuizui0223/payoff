## Model and identities
Inherited fixed C and information-following F policies, same Wave 22 payoffs. Write b_i=q_i-.5-c_i, m=.5-2(q_A-.5)(q_B-.5), J=k(1-m), s_i=b_i-k/2. Scale B's log relative fitness by beta_B: Delta_A=s_A+J*p_B, Delta_B=beta_B*(s_B+J*p_A). The sign and threshold do not change when beta_B>0; selection intensity, not literal generation time or measured evolvability, changes. Synchronous Wright-Fisher and symmetric mutation as Wave 23. Mean mismatch M=.5*(p_A+p_B)-(1-m)*p_A*p_B. Signed joint payoff deficit D=2*(.5+k)-W_joint=(k-b_A)*p_A+(k-b_B)*p_B-2*J*p_A*p_B. Equivalently D is the weighted mean of deficits at CC, FC, CF, FF. Negative is improvement, not a loss. Average over generations 1..4000 and all replicates. Endpoint FF deficit=2*k*m-b_A-b_B. These are expected payoffs on an assumed common scale, not demographic growth.

In the delayed-invasion regime s_B<0<s_B+J, p_A*=-s_B/J. Stronger beta_B increases the selective disadvantage of F before p_A*, but increases its advantage afterward. In a deterministic discrete trajectory with mu=0, logit(p_B(t+1))-logit(p_B(t))=beta_B*(s_B+J*p_A(t)). At fixed A trajectory, the prethreshold cumulative negative logit change scales exactly with beta_B. The actual coupled A trajectory may also change; no unconditional monotonicity theorem for fixation. At finite N, zero is absorbing when mu=0. If A is fixed, D=(k-b_A)+(k-b_B-2J)*p_B; where D_FC>0>D_FF, a sufficiently large B occupancy is necessary to turn a net deficit into benefit. For qB=.6,k=.3, D_FC=.02,D_FF=-.008 and the threshold conditional on A fixed is p_B>.02/.028=.714286. This is an accounting identity, not the exact threshold for the entire nonstationary trajectory.

## Main results
N=10000, qB=.8,k=.5 (FF deficit=-.2, B initial selection s_B=-.07, final +.30). Without mutation, two seed totals of joint95 ever at beta .25/1/4: 216/512,15/512,0/512. Of A-crossing runs, B reached zero strictly before A crossed in 205/511,485/512,512/512 respectively. Mean signed deficit .0442645,.199537,.211293 across seeds. The corresponding deterministic no-mutation recurrence reaches joint95 at generations 274,201,183: faster B reaches it sooner deterministically, yet much less often with drift. At mu=1e-5 all 512 runs reach joint95 for every beta; mean deficits -.179327,-.1868935,-.190386. This comparison does not demonstrate mutation effect on species survival.

N=10000, qB=.6,k=.3 (FF deficit=-.008). At mu=1e-5, joint95 counts 9,406,512 of 512; time-averaged signed deficits .0176965,.008210,-.002975, respectively. The final FF benefit is unchanged; actual cumulative payoff flips sign with selection intensity and finite supply. At qB=.6,k=.5 the final FF deficit remains positive .16: faster B with mutation shortens the more damaging FC period, not a restoration of CC. At qB=.8,k=.3 both species have positive initial F invasion margins; stronger B improves joint95 in the tested no-mutation N=1000 comparison (186,317,458 of 512). Retain all cells, including qB=.6,k=.3 no-mutation cases where joint95 is rare even with weak B selection and slow B cannot establish by the finite horizon.
