## Why this matters to GEB readers — keep this paragraph under 250 words

Biologging has produced increasingly detailed accounts of how animals track seasonal environments, but comparisons across systems remain difficult because studies use different outcomes—arrival mismatch, migration speed, stopover duration or resource-surfing scores. We introduce a common empirical phase-retention coordinate that measures how much animal–environment timing deviation remains after an ecologically meaningful movement opportunity, while estimating environmental timing innovation separately. A broad 55-species bird analysis first rejects the stronger hypothesis of one universal natural movement optimum. Direct reconstructions in mule deer, barnacle geese and Eurasian wigeon then show that phase contraction is portable across taxa but highly heterogeneous in strength and behavioral implementation. A preregistered third-taxon test supports weak contraction while falsifying a stronger near-reset forecast. Multi-flyway analyses further separate environmental information from realized correction, and an industrial-development perturbation identifies an actuation contrast while rejecting the predicted additional temporal deterioration. The study therefore uses biologging to scale from individual movement decisions to a comparative macroecological framework without forcing heterogeneous taxa into one universal mechanism.
