# PAYOFF-B tracking theory — journal targeting memo

Updated: **2026-09-24**

Status: strategy memo only. This file does not change the scientific freeze.

## First-shot: Oikos

Current journal positioning:

Oikos states that it publishes innovative empirical **and theoretical**
ecological research across taxa, systems and disciplines, with emphasis on
ecological mechanisms, processes, patterns and ideas that shape ecological
thinking.

Official source:

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/16000706

### Why the tracking paper fits

The manuscript is mechanism-first rather than system-first:

- endpoint mismatch does not identify tracking architecture;
- exact local movement/timing substitution supplies that null;
- finite temporal buffering is followed by spatial re-entry;
- landscape geometry changes the cost of maintaining correction;
- partner dependence creates a coordination failure mode;
- demographic visibility and finite-N crossing remain downstream layers.

The core result is an ecological inference mechanism: a small mismatch can hide
a large and changing tracking burden. The explicit limits of that buffering
make the mechanism testable beyond the synthetic model.

### Framing for an Oikos submission

Lead with:

> Small environmental mismatch does not identify the tracking architecture that
> keeps it small.

Keep climate change as the motivating moving-environment case. Present temporal
bypass and spatial re-entry as the primary buffering mechanism, and the
coordination barrier as a partner-dependent failure mode.

Do not lead with:

- synthetic barrier frequencies;
- arbitrary model parameter values;
- the number of implemented robustness sweeps.

## Strong fallback: Theoretical Ecology

Current journal positioning:

Theoretical Ecology explicitly welcomes theoretical work across ecological
subfields, from conceptual and computational approaches to mathematical
arguments, when theory answers ecological questions and remains readable by a
broad ecological audience.

Official Springer scope language is summarized in:

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12080-010-0070-4

### Why the tracking paper fits

This is the cleanest disciplinary fit because the manuscript contains:

- an exact analytic null;
- computational landscapes;
- evolutionary accessibility analysis;
- demographic and finite-population extensions;
- explicit ecological interpretation.

The submission would require little scientific reframing.

### Why it is second rather than first

The current manuscript already has enough mechanism-level breadth to justify a
broader ecology first shot. Theoretical Ecology is the natural fallback if a
broader journal judges the work too model-specific or insufficiently empirical.

## Stretch option: Global Change Biology

Current journal positioning:

Global Change Biology accepts theoretical and modeling studies of biological
responses to global environmental change, including adaptation, evolutionary
processes, system connectivity and climate change.

Official sources:

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/13652486/homepage/productinformation.html

https://www.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/13652486/homepage/forauthors.html

### Why the tracking paper could fit

The paper directly concerns:

- responses to directional climate forcing;
- range/phenology tracking;
- persistence under moving environments;
- adaptation and evolutionary constraint;
- connectivity and interacting species.

### Main editorial risk

The model is deliberately synthetic and uncalibrated. GCB accepts theory, but
the paper would need to persuade editors that the mechanism changes how
global-change biologists interpret observed movement/phenology combinations,
rather than simply providing another climate-tracking model.

A GCB first shot becomes more defensible only if the cover letter and
Introduction emphasize broad empirical predictions:

- similar endpoint mismatch can conceal different movement/timing allocations;
- apparent short-term timing compensation can precede renewed spatial demand;
- stronger partner dependence can block a required reallocation despite a
  viable joint solution;
- abundance data may miss real evolutionary constraints away from persistence
  boundaries.

No new empirical calibration should be added merely to chase journal prestige.

## Recommended submission sequence

```text
Oikos
  -> Theoretical Ecology
  -> Evolutionary Ecology / another specialist fallback if needed
```

Treat Global Change Biology as a deliberate stretch submission, not the default
route.

## Manuscript changes allowed for journal targeting

Allowed without reopening the scientific freeze:

- reorder Introduction emphasis;
- shorten or expand Discussion for readership;
- adapt title/abstract length;
- move robustness details between main text and supplement;
- change reference style;
- adapt figure dimensions and typography;
- journal-specific cover-letter language.

Not allowed without reopening claim review:

- adding natural prevalence language;
- presenting synthetic climate velocity as empirical;
- turning the failed demographic replication into a positive large-effect
  claim;
- importing GEB empirical phase-retention results into this paper;
- claiming novelty for the existence of combined range and phenology shifts.

## Decision rule

Unless a target-journal check reveals a format incompatibility, prepare the
first journal-specific package for **Oikos** while retaining a nearly
zero-friction fallback to **Theoretical Ecology**.
