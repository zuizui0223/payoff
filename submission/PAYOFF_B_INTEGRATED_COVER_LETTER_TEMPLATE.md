# Cover-letter template — integrated tracking ecology paper

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Hidden tracking: why environmental mismatch
does not reveal how organisms keep pace with changing environments,”** for
consideration as a research article.

The manuscript addresses a simple inference problem with broad consequences for
climate-response ecology: a small observed environmental mismatch does not
identify the tracking architecture that produced it. We combine an exact local
movement–timing substitution null, explicit moving-landscape simulations, a
registered 55-species migration analysis, and direct phase-control
reconstructions in mule deer, barnacle geese and Eurasian wigeon.

The empirical generality test is deliberately falsifiable. Across 5,816
observations from 55 migratory bird species, the data do not support one
portable natural movement-speed/environmental-wave-speed optimum. The direct
systems then explain what replaces that failed universal rule: phase error can
be transformed, but ecological interval scale and actuator architecture differ
among systems. A preregistered within-taxon industrial-mule-deer perturbation is
handled through an outcome-blind insertion contract and is not used to retune
the manuscript narrative.

The novelty claim is intentionally narrow. We do not claim novelty for
phenological mismatch, space–time climate responses, green-wave tracking,
compensatory movement, environmental predictability effects, or generic
negative-feedback mathematics. The contribution is the formal separation of
endpoint mismatch from its hidden tracking architecture, followed by explicit
capacity-, geometry-, coordination-, information- and actuation-related limits
to that buffering.

All quantitative claims are tied to frozen machine receipts. Synthetic
parameter-grid frequencies are not interpreted as natural prevalence, and raw
phase-retention coefficients are not pooled across incompatible ecological
intervals.
