## Title

**Environmental predictability alone does not determine seasonal tracking**

Article type: **Major Article**

## Abstract

Environmental predictability should help organisms track seasonal change, but
better information may arrive after opportunities to act have disappeared. We
separate environmental information quality from biological actionability in a
serial timer–controller model: a developmental or physiological timer sets
entry into a seasonal trajectory, and a downstream controller determines
whether phase error is corrected. In a prospectively specified analysis of 166
source–destination spatial pairs used by 28 migratory bird species, detrended
spring predictive connectivity increased between 2002–2009 and 2010–2017
(mean delta-rho = +0.369, 95% bootstrap CI +0.298 to +0.436; 26/28 species
means positive). Yet larger connectivity gains did not predict larger
reductions in arrival–green-up mismatch (beta = +0.062, 95% CI -0.014 to
+0.136), and structural nulls reproduced the apparent positive slope. In mule
deer, nutritional condition predicted migration-start timing, whereas signed
phase predicted downstream speed and stopover use. Together, these results
show why environmental predictability alone does not determine realized
seasonal tracking: adaptation also depends on when organisms can act and how
much downstream correction remains possible.
