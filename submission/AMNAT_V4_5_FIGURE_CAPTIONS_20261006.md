# American Naturalist V4.5 figure captions — 2026-10-06

## Figure 1. Forecastability, access, actionability and correction are distinct

Seasonal tracking is represented as four separable layers. External
forecastability quantifies prediction loss reducible by an ideal observer;
organisms may access only part of that information. In the declared reduced
model, usable information is the product of organismally accessible
decision-value and retained actionability, minus waiting cost. Residual signed
phase error can then be altered by downstream responses. The prediction and
value-of-information identities shown are established results; the figure
organizes them into the seasonal architecture tested here.

## Figure 2. Environmental forecastability increased without proportional arrival adjustment

(A) Preregistered source-destination correlation increased from 0.284 to 0.653.
(B) Posthoc target variability increased while the cross-validated
forecast-value proxy rose from -16.1 to +16.0 d². (C) In the restricted
stage subset, source mid-green-up was not consistently an online event before
the population front reached the mapped cells. (D) Target green-up advanced
2.31 d while estimated bird arrival shifted 0.19 d, moving signed
arrival-minus-green-up timing by +2.12 d. Posthoc diagnostics do not establish
cue use by birds.

## Figure 3. Individual trajectories can correct signed phase after commitment

Red Desert mule deer provide an independent individual-level correction anchor.
Across 152 animal-years, signed Days-From-Peak SD declined from 26.41 to
13.17 d (variance ratio 0.249), 70.4% ended closer to peak green-up, and mean
absolute phase error declined from 21.91 to 11.12 d. More positive starting
phase predicted faster movement (+0.0683 km d^-1 per phase day) and less
stopover use (-0.492 d per phase day). Compensation and resynchronization were
reported previously; the present reanalysis places them on a continuous signed
phase scale.
