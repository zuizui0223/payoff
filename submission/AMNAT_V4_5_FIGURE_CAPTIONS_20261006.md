# American Naturalist V4.5 figure captions — 2026-10-06

## Figure 1. Forecastability, access, actionability and correction are distinct

Conceptual hierarchy for seasonal tracking. (A) External environmental
forecastability can be expressed as reduction in prediction loss for an ideal
observer. Under Gaussian linear prediction and squared loss, baseline risk is
sigma_Y^2, ideal-observer forecast value is sigma_Y^2 rho^2, and residual risk
is sigma_Y^2(1-rho^2). These are standard prediction identities, not new
theorems. (B) Organisms may have access to only a subset of the environmental
information available to an analyst; under the same loss/action problem,
organismally accessible information value cannot exceed ideal-observer value.
(C) In the declared reduced seasonal model, usable information is
N(t)=r(t)G_O(t)-C(t), where G_O(t) is organismally accessible decision value,
r(t) retained actionability and C(t) waiting cost. Increasing accessible
information and declining actionability can generate an intermediate maximum in
usable value. (D) After commitment, residual signed phase error can be modified
by later information and actuator responses. The figure is conceptual and
combines established value-of-information and feedback-control ideas in the
seasonal architecture used here.

## Figure 2. Environmental forecastability increased without proportional arrival adjustment

Bird analyses separate environmental structure from biological information
access and population timing. (A) **Preregistered result.** Mean detrended
source-destination spring correlation increased from 0.284 in 2002–2009 to
0.653 in 2010–2017 (mean delta-rho=+0.369; original pair-bootstrap 95% CI
+0.298 to +0.436; 26/28 species means positive). (B) **Posthoc metric-scale
diagnostics.** Target green-up anomaly SD increased from 2.41 to 4.66 d.
The cross-validated forecast-value proxy
G_CV=MSE(target-history baseline)-MSE(source-informed analyst forecast)
changed from -16.1 to +16.0 d^2 (delta +32.1 d^2); 139/166 spatial pairs and
25/28 species showed positive change. G_CV is a finite-sample model-comparison
quantity and is not organismal value of information. (C) **Posthoc
observability boundary.** In the restricted 31-pair/14-species stage subset,
the realized source mid-green-up event occurred before source-front arrival in
29.8% of early and 45.8% of late annual observations, between source- and
target-front arrival in 29.6% and 18.3%, and after target-front arrival in
41.6% and 36.1%. Thus the reconstructed predictor was retrospectively
forecastable but was not demonstrated to be an online cue at the mapped source
stage. (D) **Posthoc signed timing decomposition.** Target green-up advanced
from day 130.98 to 128.67 (shift -2.31 d), whereas estimated bird arrival
changed from day 123.17 to 122.98 (shift -0.19 d). Signed
arrival-minus-green-up timing shifted from -7.81 to -5.69 d (+2.12 d; 95% CI
+1.41 to +2.77). Zero lag is not assumed to be a fitness optimum.

## Figure 3. Individual trajectories can correct signed phase after commitment

Red Desert mule deer provide an independent individual-level anchor for the
downstream-correction layer. Ortega et al. (2023) previously documented
bidirectional compensation and resynchronization; the public-source-data
reanalysis here places that phenomenon on the continuous phase scale used in
the framework. (A) Across 152 animal-years from 72 adult females, signed
Days-From-Peak SD declined from 26.41 d at migration start to 13.17 d at the
end; the end/start variance ratio was 0.249 (95% animal-cluster bootstrap CI
0.167–0.362). (B) Seventy point four percent of animal-years ended closer to
peak green-up than they started, and mean absolute phase error declined from
21.91 to 11.12 d. Whole-route phase retention was lambda=0.107 (95% CI
0.013–0.209). (C) Each additional day of positive starting phase was
associated with +0.0683 km d^-1 movement rate (95% CI +0.0554 to +0.0800)
and -0.492 d stopover use (95% CI -0.569 to -0.412), so late individuals
moved faster and stopped less while early individuals showed the opposite
adjustment. (D) The individual-level pattern motivates the serial
phase-correction layer but does not identify the mechanism operating in the
bird system.
